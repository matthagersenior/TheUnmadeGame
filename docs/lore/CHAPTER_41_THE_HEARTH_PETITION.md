# Chapter 41 — The Hearth Petition

*Optional Cinderhold household investigation. No claimant's signature can
convert somebody else's wages into the hero's reward.*

## A book beside the warmth

Bes keeps the missed-wage book on a pay desk inside his real home. The book is
not proof that every person who has heard the story has visited his hearth.
The player may read it only after Bes has reported unpaid wages and the home
has actually been visited. This step adds no pay, heat, reputation or reward.

Rheva has independently checked the municipal record. Only after hearing her
corroboration may the traveler examine the civic ledger outside the household.
The ledger admits an uncomfortable truth: the town has an obligation that its
private kindness has not discharged.

## Two petitions, not two victories

At separate petition tables the player can choose one of two lasting courses.
Both require a second deliberate interaction, at the same physical table,
within twelve active-world seconds. There is no default choice.

**The confidential petition** preserves household and refuge privacy. A sealed
record appears in Cinderhold; no public list of guests is invented. The debt
remains real, and the shelter's privacy is not evidence that the claim was
settled.

**The redacted public hearing** places a legible claim before the city, without
exposing refuge guests. The docket is a request to account for owed wages,
not a conviction, a financial transfer or automatic solidarity. Its different
physical record remains visible in the settlement.

Neither branch creates money, erases the unsettled debt, awards a weapon,
forces Mera to lend, declares a victor or revises the earlier Unpaid Shift.
Only one record survives. The chosen ending cannot be overwritten by later
visits or by replaying the other choice station.

## The following day

The player may return to the outcome marker, but the monument alone has
no voice. On a later in-world day the traveler must speak personally to Bes
to receive his reaction. Until that conversation, the world must not report
that Bes knows about an acknowledgment he has not given.

If the record was confidential, Bes recognizes the protected household claim
while insisting that privacy does not repay wages. If it was made public, he
asks the city to answer the ledger without making a spectacle of his home.
Rheva can speak about the filed record only after the actual next-day return;
Ovenna and Mera remain limited to facts they personally know.

### Future return possibilities

The petition is deliberately separate from actual repayment, walkout duration,
local supplies and third-party guarantees. Those existing systems can continue
changing on later visits; the petition remains an attributable choice without
claiming those other obligations are solved. The unresolved civic tension may
support future expansions, but neither course predetermines Nhal-Vey's answer.
