# THE UNMADE — VOLUME X: THE UNANSWERED ATLAS

### A primary storyworld volume, not merely a companion manual

*New literary canon and future-adaptation reference · 10 October 2026 · Preproduction edition*

> "A history is not a place where the dead live. It is a door the living decide to open."

The Unmade is not the story of a chosen heir instructed to defeat a god. It is a universe in which ordinary people have made practical use of impossible omissions. Someone withheld a sound; another society sold the absence as tomorrow's debt; others hung their houses from a missing note, built a wall from an erased lineage, counted citizens who could not have been born, and obeyed a crown that no one accepted. The problem is not that everything is fake. The problem is that everything has become *real to someone*.

This volume supplies a living literary foundation for stories capable of standing without the player protagonist. It fixes recognizable civic worlds, performers' character anchors, motifs, reliable callbacks, and unresolved questions. The storytelling contract is humane and exact: you may create a refuge without erasing an archive; you may reveal exploitation without exposing someone who has chosen privacy. Neither concern becomes an easy morality button.

**Continuity classification:** The names and stable IDs cited here are already present in the game sources. The new physical details, private character motivations, and extended callbacks are **new editorial canon for future writing**, not automatically implemented Unreal scenes, recorded dialogue, or changed save outcomes. A character's theory is not a narrator's fact. An OPEN mystery stays open until a deliberate, separately reviewed lore decision.

## 1. The narrative promise

Every book, episode, film, optional quest, lore codex, or future expansion follows six rules:

1. **Make the place unforgettable.** A viewer who sees Saltwake's skeletal piers beneath pounding rain should know the city before any title card. Its missing sea has civic consequences, not only strange lighting.
2. **Give every human a private life.** Hessa's challenge is not "give quest"; it is that her refuge's secrecy protects people and can also deny them the justice they need. Yllesh's fear is real; so is Orru's refusal.
3. **Remember who knows what.** No character can repeat the player's private choices in another realm without firsthand evidence, public publication, or a delivered account. Delayed or changed couriers can create dramatic consequences, but not magic omniscience.
4. **Answer local mysteries, keep larger ones honest.** Repair the ferry, protect the singers' rest, contest a debt. The sealed chart's author, the unoccupied lectern's handprint and the second impossible footsteps may remain unanswered.
5. **Let callbacks change meaning.** The refuge cup begins as a kindness, returns as documentary proof of a hidden household, and becomes an image of voluntary remembrance. Merely hiding the same symbol in backgrounds is not storytelling.
6. **Protect the player-created protagonist.** The novels and screen stories can use Hessa, Sella, Yllesh or Issil as protagonists. They cannot decree a single canon player sex, race, heritage, morality or mutually exclusive postboss future.

### The signature grammar

**The Unanswered Interval** is usually not audible as silence. It is what becomes *conspicuously missing amid normal life*: the absent end of a bell note under the clatter of cups; no wave-slap behind rain; a furnace burn without fuel; seven rests recorded in a six-part choir. Never cut every ambient sound merely to tell an audience they should feel uneasy. Let a character's hand or eye betray what they expected to hear.

**Social objects are keys to memory**, not meaningless collectibles: the uncounted cup, dry rope knot, cooled coal, backward ledger, broken tuning fork, protected name-rubbing, voluntarily tied doorway cord, untouched orchard fruit, and departure footprint.

**An answer costs something.** It can cost a reputation, a livelihood, a public promise or the comfort of an institution. It should never require nonconsensual exposure as an unchallenged narrative reward.

## 2. A city must be instantly identifiable

These eleven settlements inherit their exact canonical names and realm assignments from the living-world atlas. Their following shapes, sounds, civic disputes and repeatable images are now the adaptation keys. A local episode has to begin in *that* city rather than interchangeable medieval streets.

### 1. The Crossings — three roads meet under one missing mile

**Silhouette:** Timber footbridges tied with three competing knots; street tiles from three abandoned maps. The unmistakable emblem is **three mismatched road knots**.

**What an audience hears:** A humming roadstone counterpoint to market kettles. **How people behave:** Wards refuse to ask a traveler for a name until they have eaten.

**Power and friction:** Bridge Ledger Court. Orrel marks crossings with an inkless nail; Kesta can deliver testimony but cannot decide which road is right

**The unsettled incident:** The third road runs through an address erased from Paperhaven's publicly dated surveys.

**Adaptation guard:** Keep the city's material signature, audible identity, civic disagreement, and visible ordinary life; never make its paradox a generic fantasy portal.

## 3. The people who can carry a novel or television season

A camera should recognize a returning Unmade character by silhouette, gesture, habit, and the decision they cannot easily make—not by title cards or mystical exposition. These thirteen leading identities are drawn from the 82 already registered residents. Their original stable actor IDs are unchanged. No new character here replaces an established person.

### 1. Hessa the refuge matron

*Persistent ID: `npc.bellwold.matron.001`*

**Recognizable before a name is spoken:** One thumb permanently marked by hot kettle handles; refuses to stand behind the registration desk. Their recurring prop is **an uncounted porcelain cup, chipped on its lip**.

**Public responsibility:** Keep the refuge open through a second night of danger. **Private hunger:** Be believed without having to surrender the protected names of the people she saved. **Human failing:** Quietly decides what others are permitted to know, even when the secrecy becomes paternalism.

**The old wound:** A child she sheltered was once made into a census entry against her promise. **Voice and performance:** Warm contralto; ends arguments by asking whether someone has eaten.

**What they tell themselves:** “If nobody writes down a name, nobody can use it to hurt them.” **What their story tests:** Her mercy can conceal harms that records might prevent.

**Two living bonds:** Sorin wants triage first; she asks him to make room for one more traveler (`npc.bellwold.healer.001`); Ivera asks her to admit when protection becomes silence (`npc.bellwold.childtutor.001`); Sevrin distrusts unverified refuge numbers (`npc.paperhaven.registrar.001`).

**A first line:** “We keep one cup uncounted. It is not missing.” **When we return:** After a later return, she carries the same cup but lets its owner decide where to sit.

**Question that stays:** Why does the cup's hairline crack echo the uncast bell's seam?

### 2. Ivera the tutor

*Persistent ID: `npc.bellwold.childtutor.001`*

**Recognizable before a name is spoken:** Chalk dust up to one wrist; looks at people's shoes when adults discuss who belongs. Their recurring prop is **a lesson slate scored with seven lines but only six numerals**.

**Public responsibility:** Teach the displaced children to read public records critically. **Private hunger:** Protect pupils without teaching them that facts are optional. **Human failing:** Assumes disclosure is always kinder if written clearly.

**The old wound:** An absent name in a class register belonged to a child she knew. **Voice and performance:** Fast precise phrasing that slows around protected testimony.

**What they tell themselves:** “Any true entry ought to be public.” **What their story tests:** A right to truth can threaten a right to remain anonymous.

**Two living bonds:** Hessa once hid the very document she needs to teach with (`npc.bellwold.matron.001`); Sorin witnesses the consequences of Ivera's certainty (`npc.bellwold.healer.001`).

**A first line:** “If the number is wrong, why is the child still here?” **When we return:** On return she teaches the same lesson with a blank line deliberately left intact.

**Question that stays:** The seventh slate line appears on stones she has never visited.

## 4. Original literary scene: The Uncounted Cup

*Bellwold. A first-volume opening scene. This is narrative source, not an in-engine cutscene.*

The bell failed to ring while Hessa was pouring the last cup.

It was not the silence that troubled her. At this hour the refuge made every ordinary sound of survival: pots catching each other in a sink, the dragging cough of old Corvin returning from the masonry yard, a child asking whether tomorrow had a proper name. Beyond the shelter walls, the tower kept its empty place above the roofs. Those who had known Bellwold longer than its official records said a bell had once hung there. The records said no metal had ever been cast.

Hessa did not care what the records said before breakfast.

She counted eight bowls on the table. There were seven people seated. Then she filled the ninth cup, the chipped white one she never included in the tally, and left it by the door.

Ivera watched from the steps, slate beneath her arm. She had chalk on her wrist and the angry patience of a teacher who knew that an explanation was being withheld.

"Eight bowls," Ivera said.

"Seven eaters."

"Nine cups."

"One is for someone who may come."

"Or someone who did?"

Hessa turned the kettle so the hot handle faced away from the little girl nearest the fire. It was a gesture she made without thinking, years after anyone had asked her to. "Both people are welcome," she said.

Ivera did not write that down. Instead she set the slate on the table, beside the cup. Across its surface ran seven tidy horizontal lines. Only six of them carried numbers.

"This line appeared yesterday," she said.

"Chalk does not appear."

"Children are remarkably good at chalk, yes. But this was below a sum I did not let them change. If they are lying, they're very clever. If they aren't—"

"Perhaps you should ask what the seventh line means to them."

"I did. They said it was where the bell went."

At that moment the watchman opened the door. A traveler stood behind him with a child's cloak bundled against one shoulder. Rain had touched the traveler's shoes but not the cloak. Their face was the face of anyone who had walked farther than they intended and could not be sure the road behind them would still be there by afternoon.

"Have you a place?" the watchman asked.

Hessa lifted the uncounted cup. "We've a chair."

"I don't want to be entered," said the traveler.

Ivera put a hand on her slate.

"No one asked for your name," Hessa answered.

"You will eventually."

"I might. You needn't give it."

The traveler looked toward the bell tower. In the space where its last note should have hung, the market outside continued calling prices. A child laughed. A horse knocked its shoe against a cobble. For an interval no longer than a held breath, the traveler appeared to know a place no one had named.

The cloak shifted. Under it the bundled shape was only a rolled blanket.

Hessa saw Ivera see it too.

For the first time that morning, neither woman knew whether kindness or evidence ought to speak first.

The traveler took the cup. There was a fine crack in the porcelain, running from the chipped lip toward the base. Ivera would later insist she had seen that same shape in the official rubbing of a bell that had never been cast. Hessa would remember only the traveler's thumb resting across it, as if the cup might open.

When the traveler asked where to put the blanket, Hessa showed them an empty bed.

By noon, the bed was warm.

By night, the guest was gone. Nobody had heard the door.

The cup remained. It was the last thing Hessa washed, and the first thing she set out again.

**Knowledge ledger:** Hessa witnessed the traveler and the empty blanket. Ivera saw the crack and believed it matched a rubbing; she has not proved that connection. No page confirms the traveler's identity or turns the scene into a hidden royal-origin prophecy. Both characters remain ethically legible.

## 5. Original literary scene: The Seventh Rest

*Orravane. A later stand-alone story opening, several generations of choral engineering into the city's inhabited history.*

The day Orravane nearly fell, nobody screamed.

The singers did what they had been trained to do since childhood. They widened their stance, touched the rail with two fingers, and gave the supporting note as a single note should be given: steady, without the small vanity of ornament, with enough breath behind it to keep the hanging street from shifting. Beneath them, the lower houses waited in the wrong direction. Soil sprinkled upward from a garden bed.

There were six throats at the rail. Yllesh heard seven voices.

He knew what every one of those singers could bear. He kept the chart himself: morning pitches, evening recoveries, the faint tremor that entered an exhausted voice three breaths before the singer admitted they needed rest. The seventh sound belonged to no name on his chart. It had come before, sometimes when all six held their pitch and sometimes during an authorized pause. He had stopped recording it years ago.

Orru stood at the end of the rail, arms crossed. The empty seventh lectern was between them.

"Dismiss us," Orru said.

"The east houses are still moving."

"We can hear them. They always move. Dismiss us."

Yllesh followed the sound into the joints above the lower stair. Somewhere inside the timber a load was traveling without a beam to carry it. A hundred people lived beneath that stair. Orru knew it. So did every singer whose mouth had gone pale from holding a note.

"If we stop now—"

"That is what you always say before we stop."

"Look at the marks on the rail."

"I put those marks there."

Yllesh knew. Orru had scratched one line for each day a promised rest had been postponed. The scratches ran almost the entire length of the rail. Every time he reached their beginning he told himself the city had survived because he had been strong enough to be cruel.

"One more phrase," he said.

Orru did not sing.

The other five kept their tone. One by one their mouths began to shake, not from terror but from exhaustion held too long for pride. A roof shifted. The floor pressed lightly upward against Yllesh's boots, as though the world were trying to stand where he stood.

Then Orru placed a hand upon the seventh lectern.

The missing note stopped.

A support bead slid along its wire and came to rest in the vacant socket. The east houses did not fall. They settled a hand's breadth lower and stayed there.

Yllesh waited for the scream, the crack, the unmistakable sound of the city proving him right.

Instead he heard one singer breathe. Then another. A window opened below them and someone shouted up to ask why it had suddenly become possible to hear the rain.

"Rest," Orru told the others.

Only when they had gone did Yllesh inspect the lectern. Beneath the polished rim, where there should have been no human wear, a shallow groove had been made by a hand resting in the same place for a long time. The wood was warm.

He kept both discoveries to himself until dawn. He told the engineers about the bead. He had always been able to speak of weight.

He did not tell them about the hand.

**Knowledge ledger:** Yllesh witnessed the load settling and later examined a worn lectern; Orru felt the mechanism respond. Neither knows whether a person, a memory, or a pressure phenomenon produced it. This mystery does not absolve Yllesh of earlier coercion or require singers to become magical fuel.

## 10. A film need not tell the same story as the series

A self-contained film can follow **The Seventh Rest**: a choral-engineering disaster story with Orru, Yllesh and the city's tired families; worldbuilding is revealed by labor, architecture, consent and a missing sound. A novel can instead center **The Cup No Census Claimed**, a moral mystery about hiding a person's identity and preserving the documents necessary to protect them. Its ending does not certify one universal backstory for the player hero.

## 11. The rules for expansions, cameos and adaptations

1. Characters may have medium-specific dialogue but their personal motives, name, origin region and limits on knowledge remain consistent.
2. No series/novel/movie declares one mutually exclusive player ending the universal canonical ending; adapt with a visible variant label or set before the divergence.
3. A local humane victory must not claim that a cosmic villain personally perpetrated every historical injustice.
4. A mystery may yield a satisfying local answer while its larger pattern remains legitimately unanswered.
5. Distinctive icons, sound motifs, materials and gestures recur naturally; no arbitrary Easter eggs without a provenance path.
6. The protagonist's appearance, ancestry and chosen ethics remain free; side protagonists can carry standalone screen stories.

**Canon and evidence hierarchy.** An original game-state save, quest gate or fixed actor ID cannot be overturned by a prose interpretation. A source-observed event outranks a rumor. A writer may depict honest incompatible witnesses; they cannot invent an answer by declaring one witness unknowingly omniscient. The two post-Nhal-Vey futures are both legitimate variants, not 'right' and 'fake'.

**Mystery release discipline.** OPEN mysteries remain open in scripts, promo copy, AI dialogue, branching side content and supplementary fiction. To resolve one requires a separate reviewed canon decision: what evidence was already present, which people learn it, what existing events change, how future media callbacks work, and which parts remain unknowable.

**Production truth.** This is *new editorial primary-source storyworld material* attached to stable source identifiers. It is not a UE-compiled game, a completed professionally voiced film, or a promise that every scene here has collision, animation or dialog code. Future assets are traceable by city, character, mystery and callback IDs.

## 17. The Witness Braid — An Inquiry That Refuses a Crown
*Volume X playable interlude · October 10, 2026 · new source-implemented optional storyline*

The Crossings had three roads and three surveyors. Orrel used to say that when all three disagreed, they had at least proved there was ground beneath their feet. Then a blue-corded nail began trembling whenever a traveler said they had come from a place the courts no longer acknowledged.

**The Witness Braid** is what Orrel calls the work of setting incompatible firsthand objects side by side without taking possession of their people. Its principal antagonists are not monsters. They are the ordinary pressure to make a record convenient for the institution reading it, and the equally dangerous temptation to hide a record that could protect someone else.

### Episode scene: three cities, one interval

At **Bellwold**, Hessa's uncounted refuge cup is chipped on one side. She is not sure when it broke. She refuses to have her guests treated as the evidence needed to date it. The public Afterlight choice may make her cup a sign of shelter or documentary testimony, but does not reveal a missing bell-maker.

At **Paperhaven**, Sevrin keeps the reverse of a seal press blank. The Index Hall can add an annotation to a record without publishing a sleeping guest's name. The press bears a date that, according to the most careful archivists, should precede the press's own use. The player is not entitled to conclude that the guest is the impossible person.

At **the Crossings**, Orrel's inkless nail marks a third road, yet the official surveys differ about whether that road ever had a place in the city. Kesta may eventually carry an account to another neighborhood, but only as testimony she could physically deliver. She is not an omniscient courier.

Nothing about these three objects establishes a cosmic verdict. Each is a separate, locally witnessed trace. **Only after personally inspecting all three and returning to at least one after a legitimate local story outcome has changed its meaning** can the player make a public response at Orrel's nail. No book, prophecy, paid upgrade or guardian boss bypasses those observations.

### A choice with architecture, not a cosmetic dialogue reward

The same familiar F7 and F8 controls used by other irreversible decisions require two matching presses inside the game's six-second commitment gate. The player must be physically beside the inkless nail. If a final local quest witness takes priority, that earlier obligation is resolved through its original flow; the Braid never steals another quest's choice.

**F7 — The Sheltered Thread.** A low, horizontal refuge cord becomes physically present nearby. It guides unnamed travelers toward an established shelter without claiming who owns their histories. That protection comes at a cost: Sevrin cannot easily present the complete pattern in the public archive. Future writing can revisit someone excluded from a hearing because the lead remained private. The source currently shows the structure and journals the result; it does not yet implement those later conversations or city-wide AI navigation.

**F8 — The Public Docket.** A taller public record appears instead. It contains the *objects' contradictions* but no protected individuals' names. A witness can challenge an institution with this evidence, yet hostile readers might begin hunting the people who were never named. Orrel fears what publishing even a redacted question could invite. The source currently shows the record, writes the choice and reports a local player event; it does not simulate distant reprisals that have not yet been authored.

Both remain valid, mutually exclusive save outcomes. Neither produces a magical reward, forces an ending or tells the player they chose evil. The named NPCs continue to have private lives outside the protagonist's decision.

### Why the mystery remains alive

There are at least three incompatible causal hypotheses, each belonging to a **speaker**, not to an authoritative omniscient narrator:

- **Hessa's account:** the cup was chipped by a guest who has been erased from the count. This would make refuge an intervention in history, but she cannot prove when it happened.
- **Sevrin's account:** the inked side of the seal was made during a routine records correction, while the undated side is a later form of intentional consent. This would make the missing entry a policy choice rather than a temporal rupture.
- **Orrel's account:** a road that was taxed before it was built exposed the contradiction, and the records adapted afterward. That suggests the map failed first, but his nail cannot provide a date.

No automatically awarded quest text chooses among these hypotheses. They are prompts for later revisits, dialogue, an anthology episode, and optional future expansions. The broader nine-realm unresolved questions, customizable protagonist origin, Nhal-Vey's false victory and both alternate mornings are unchanged.

## 18. The Living Evidence — The Second Truth Is That Someone Told You
*Volume X playable / adaptation continuity chapter · 10 October 2026*

The phenomenon that most unsettles Sevrin is not the archive's impossible date. It is a perfectly ordinary sentence spoken by a neighbor: **“I know because someone told me.”** The neighbor may be sincere, mistaken, courageous, afraid, or protecting somebody. The sentence is evidence of a conversation, not evidence that the conversation's account was true.

### 18.1 Three objects; three minds

**Hessa / CALL.01 — The Cup.** A refugee under her protection may have chipped the cup before being removed from public records. Hessa *suspects* this and has excellent reasons to distrust any clerk eager to publish the theory. Her private cost: protecting unnamed guests can also leave an exploited household with no paper proof of its existence. Never make the player discover a protected guest's identity as a free collectible reward.

**Sevrin / CALL.03 — The Reverse Seal.** Sevrin believes the unmarked face records a deliberate consent limit. He can demonstrate how a record can be amended without naming its vulnerable subject. But its contradictory date might precede the seal press's own manufacture. His private cost: delaying a correction can become a form of institutional self-protection; he does not always notice when he crosses that line.

**Orrel / CALL.04 — The Inkless Nail.** Orrel suspects the disputed third road was taxed before its route was agreed. He remembers carrying wood beside surveyors who disagreed over where the road lay. Three surveys can all be physically present and still contradict one another. His private cost: he owes wages to builders the city denies it employed.

A player who reads Hessa's cup does **not** automatically learn Sevrin's account. An accessible notebook has slots for only *firsthand physically inspected* relics. On returning after a legitimate local civic decision, the same object may be witnessed again under a different interpretation; that is a change in the available account, not a retroactive rewrite of the first memory.

### 18.2 The map of knowledge is not the map of the world

The evidence notebook is a record **of how the player came to know**, not a canonical verdict tree. It may show the author's claim and the best known objection. Claims must remain attributed to Hessa, Sevrin and Orrel, not to “THE UNIVERSE.” The objective facts are the physical objects, the player's authenticated inspections, saved civic outcomes and the private publication decision. All deeper causal explanations remain OPEN.

A found object has three stages:

1. **Hidden:** Until the player physically visits and inspects the scene, the object's identity, theory and location-specific objection cannot appear in their notebook.
2. **Personally observed:** The initial text, named author and challenge become visible; neither an NPC's unexplained guess nor a stale memory substitutes for a visit.
3. **Observed again:** After a valid local choice changes its public meaning, a return interaction preserves both the earlier state and the new reading. This is not proof that one was a lie.

The full cross-city comparison is unlocked only by three personal observations and one changed revisit, regardless of the order in which the player visits. An inaccessible city does not automatically grant clues. A future localization must preserve the explicit markers for *speaker, hearsay and firsthand evidence*.

### 18.3 The people do not have global quest-state vision

When the player publishes the redacted docket, an NPC who physically sees it go up can say:

> “I saw the redacted docket posted. It names no guest; I worry who will search for the people it does not name.”

That resident may become concerned even if the player's intention was benign. If a second resident hears that account from the first in person, they may say:

> “Someone told me a redacted docket was posted. I have not seen it and cannot confirm their explanation.”

They must name their source rather than pretending they stood at the Crossings. The second statement is not an eyewitness account, cannot be propagated again as one, and cannot be used to make a final quest judgment. The same distinction governs the Sheltered Thread's physical refuge cord.

**Rules of knowledge propagation:** An on-site act emits a *distinct* source event for sheltered versus public response; real local line of sight determines which residents directly observe it. An existing spatial conversation loop passes an eyewitness account to a nearby listener as **rumor** with a stable speaker ID. No absent resident, unseen distant city, generic AI output or global story flag confers the event to every NPC. Player dialogue, trust and fear may respond to the event **only if that resident has it in personal memory**.

**Limitations of today's implementation:** The graybox supports a read-only F9 notebook and Blueprint-returned text, source-limited individual dialogue and local one-hop memory propagation. It does not yet have a sophisticated evidence-wall interface, an actual delivery caravan traveling across realms, voice-acted testimony, or a next-morning scene that stages all of the affected characters. Those require later implemented releases.

### 18.4 A missing line at the end of a conversation

In a possible later scene, Kesta brings a folded docket to Hessa. Hessa notices its date before she recognizes the ink. Kesta says she heard Orrel saw the paper posted; her words deliberately stop short of claiming that **she** stood at the nail. Hessa cannot answer whether the date was wrong. She only asks how Kesta knows the person who signed it would consent to the record.

This scene is a **future dramatic beat**, not automatically present in NPC memory or playable Unreal staging. The writer may make Kesta mistaken or late, but may not secretly transform her carried hearsay into eyewitness truth. A television adaptation can dramatize the journey. A game expansion can make the player physically escort the folded testimony; if the caravan is attacked, the knowledge must be delayed rather than magically delivered.

### 18.5 Safeguards for the larger franchise

The Living Evidence is not a hidden morality score, a false simulation of deep AGI, or a replacement for authored characters. It is a mechanical promise: **the world reacts to what its people could actually know**. The nine realms, eleven distinctive settlements, 82 stable residents, ten reality disciplines, branching main campaign, Nhal-Vey's false victory and both postboss mornings remain the same canon. Writers may ask new questions without forcing the answer to the original impossible interval.

## 19. The Folded Dispatch — The Distance Between Knowing and Being Told
*Volume X optional game chapter · source-implemented physical delivery / future expansion seed*

A letter cannot walk to Bellwold because the player closed an argument in the Crossings. The road between the two towns still has its own mud, workers, customs and people who may fail to recognize an unfamiliar traveler. **The Folded Dispatch** exists so that a player can personally carry one contested civic record to someone with a reason to care.

### The place and the packet

The physical packet waits near Orrel's inkless nail after a real saved Witness Braid decision. If the player chose the **Sheltered Thread**, it carries the terms of a refuge passage without naming the people who may use it. If the player chose the **Public Docket**, it carries an account of the contradictions posted in public, already redacted to avoid publishing protected guests' names. No hidden passenger manifests and no miraculous testimony are enclosed.

It is physically handled through the ordinary **E interaction** within reach and line of sight. The world does not award the packet just because the player is nearby, reads a note in a menu or presses F9. Carrying it uses the same save, so leaving the Crossings and returning later does not erase custody. There is no inventory exploit or respawning reward for collecting the same letter repeatedly. The packet ceases to appear in the world once honestly collected.

### Bellwold's receiver

Hessa's daily work continues whether or not anyone brings a paper to her refuge. She may have heard another person's speculation, but the game must not make her describe a carried document she never received.

Only when the player reaches **Hessa in person**, can stand beside her and has an unobstructed conversation, can the actual packet be handed over. The delivery is marked with the in-world day when Hessa receives it. A player who has never collected the record, who stands near a different resident, or who refuses to travel does not satisfy the handoff.

**With the Sheltered Thread:** Hessa considers the safe passage for unnamed travelers and notices what remains unpaid: protection does not automatically provide a hearing to those excluded from past records. Her line is not generic praise.

**With the Public Docket:** She recognizes the player maintained the redactions, then asks what a fair hearing can ask without turning refuge into surveillance. Her fear has a personal basis rather than a universal reputation penalty.

Her knowledge is therefore grounded in a physical object the player actually handed her. Another resident remains uninformed until they legitimately see or hear a report themselves. The optional delivery does not unlock the main story, complete an offscreen character's arc or identify an absent child.

### A transport that matters

The first playable version keeps the player in control of the transfer. This is not a simulated autonomous Kesta courier run: Kesta can be a future character in this chain only after a specifically authored conversation and dispatch permission. No sky-wide event automatically informs the destination.

A future expansion may present a dangerous route: rain that falls where no ocean exists, a bridge where two maps disagree about a mile, or a registrar asking to read a document its carrier cannot ethically disclose. Those hazards must support fair counterplay, recovered losses, alternate approaches and saved responses; they are **not** already implemented by the current packet pickup.

### Spoken scene for adaptation

*Hessa does not reach for the packet immediately. She finishes washing the chipped cup, then holds out her clean hand.*

**Hessa:** "If that paper came from the Crossings, did you see who sent it?"

**Player-carrier, variable:** "I found it beside the nail. I brought it because you should decide what it means here."

**Hessa:** "Then I know where you found it. I do not yet know why they left it."

*She reads. The refuge lantern has not changed. A guest at the next table continues to eat. There is no musical sting, no revelation that identifies the guest, and no answer to the first missing interval.*

This scene's fully voiced/performance blocking is **editorial intent**, not live imported voice acting or filmed gameplay. The physical source actor, custody state, day stamps, conditional delivered line and persistence are the implemented portion.

### Rules for cross-realm follow-through

- Source-implemented packet is **optional**, physically spawned only after the earlier Braid resolution; collecting and handing over require real proximity/line-of-sight actions.
- Day-indexed custody stages are `Waiting`, `PlayerCarrying`, `HandDelivered`; they are validated and restored on the same backward-compatible save, with refusal on impossible time ordering or a delivery without a Braid outcome.
- On failed save writes, the previous custody stage is restored. A staged pickup cannot grant a second reward and a delivered packet cannot be collected again.
- Hessa's recipient line is restricted to her stable identity and a legitimately delivered packet. Her distinct reaction depends on which document the player transported. No unrelated NPC knows the content because a global quest state changed.
- Future physical mail routes, traveling NPC Kesta, player ambushes, actor animation, paper props, UMG display, spoken delivery, controller accessibility and full UE PIE validation remain **unbuilt**.

A world becomes memorable not when its mysteries are enormous, but when the distance between two people matters.

## 20. The Return of the Witness — Four Ways to Leave a Door Open
*Volume X, interactive return-encounter chapter · 10 October 2026*

The hardest thing about carrying a letter is the knowledge that its recipient may answer it. The player has delivered Orrel's folded record to Hessa. The world does **not** immediately give the player a quest reward or put the answer in their pocket. Hessa has a refuge to run. She has to sleep, finish the next morning's work and decide which harm she is willing to risk.

### The night that must actually pass

If the player returns to Hessa on the same in-world day as the first delivery, she says only what she already knows about the received document. She cannot have secretly written a full reply between two consecutive button presses. The investigation journal instead explains that a morning must pass. The waiting is a real world-clock prerequisite, not a purchased skip and not a forced real-time timeout. The protagonist remains free to travel, trade, fight, seek another realm's rites and return later.

The reply is **optional**. It never gates the nine-realm main campaign or the finale. An old save without the response remains valid; an impossible timestamp or a letter received before it was physically collected does not.

### Hessa's two answers and her private life

On a later in-world day, standing beside Hessa, the player chooses how Hessa's own text can travel. The familiar guarded F7/F8 interaction requires two identical choices beside the **same** physical speaker within six seconds. The warning describes the consequence before committing.

**F7 — Private Counsel.** Hessa asks Orrel to keep an unnamed crossing in use, even while public records remain incomplete. Her words are not a confession of a secret guest's identity. She says: *“A refuge cannot survive if every life becomes a witness exhibit.”* This keeps a road safe but can delay restitution for builders never acknowledged by the court.

**F8 — Public Hearing.** Hessa calls on Orrel to challenge the contradictory surveys before the Bridge Ledger Court. The document names no protected guest. She says: *“No guest's name appears, though the city may still ask after them.”* Public contradiction may create an accountable record and still invite intimidation.

The player does not magically know which decision will create a perfect outcome. Neither option is coded as good or evil; they represent different, learnable trade-offs. Hessa remains a person with work, relationships and fears, not a choice dispenser.

### The second journey must be physical

After Hessa chooses, a folded reply can be taken with **E** from a world object near her working refuge. The player must be nearby, able to see it and close enough to interact without another closer character stealing the action. The packet is then held in the existing save through later journeys and rests. The paper cannot be copied for experience points.

To complete the return, the player must stand before **Orrel**, the established bridge keeper at the Crossings, and use **E** with clear sight. The recipient is checked by Orrel's unique stable identity, not by whether the player entered the right region, hovered over a map card or spoke to an interchangeable guard.

The original Folded Dispatch's collection and receipt history remains intact. Hessa's reply stores its own chosen day, collection day and delivery day, with the second decision always strictly later than the day Hessa received the first document.

### Four intersections, none declared correct

Orrel's response has **four distinct versions** arising from the original Crossings choice and the later Hessa reply.

| First Crossings decision | Hessa's reply | What Orrel actually says or does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sheltered Thread | Private Counsel | He maintains the private crossing while admitting the unrecognized builders' wages remain unpaid |
| Public Docket | Private Counsel | He must reconcile the public disagreement already on display with Hessa's demand for an unnamed safe passage |
| Sheltered Thread | Public Hearing | The protected route enters public debate; the crossing can now both shelter and implicate its keepers |
| Public Docket | Public Hearing | A public hearing is requested without naming a single guest; the matter becomes a question rather than a verdict |

The game spawns a persistent low private-counsel marker or a taller public-hearing marker near the Crossings after an actual Orrel receipt. These are prototype physical consequences, not fully animated assemblies. A returning player can recognize what they chose even when no quest dialog is open.

**Orrel's own voice, when a private path follows the published docket:** “The public docket remains, but Hessa asks for a private passage. I must answer both obligations.”

**Orrel's own voice, when the protected route faces a hearing:** “We sheltered the travelers, then brought the contradiction to hearing. The road may protect and accuse us.”

These lines cannot appear as his firsthand knowledge until the player genuinely delivers the corresponding reply. Another resident does not learn them merely because the global source save contains a new stage.

### Echoes far beyond the Crossings

A letter moving in both directions gives the next expansion a precise ethical question: *Can a person tell the truth without giving everyone permission to own it?* Saltwake's missing sea tariff and Drevlach's invoice from a future harvest might later be compared with the Bridge Ledger Court's paper trail, but no existing script has proved the connection. Their individual civilizations must respond according to their own legal customs and material fears.

Kesta, the courier, could one day carry an authorized copy, travel a route with hazards and become a witness who can be questioned. She is not automatically dispatched in the present implementation. The player performs this journey personally.

### Cinematic, novel and television potential

*The refuge hall at dusk. Hessa keeps writing after everyone thinks the workday is finished. She stops not because of a supernatural event, but because a young guest wakes and asks for water. She fills the chipped cup, places it beside the bed and returns to the paper.*

*The next morning, the player is already outside when Hessa opens the door.*

**HESSA:** “They asked whether the road existed. Nobody asked who had been keeping it open.”

**PLAYER:** “What do you want me to take back?”

**HESSA:** “What I can answer for. Not what they want me to prove.”

*In the Crossings, Orrel reads the paper twice. A hammer strikes somewhere down the street. An ordinary builder keeps working while the survey maps disagree.*

**ORREL:** “Then the road stays in the world. The question stays with us.”

This sequence is an **authored adaptation treatment**, not already voiced or filmed. Its chronology is deliberate: Hessa's response requires living time, a physical carrier and Orrel's actual receipt.

## 21. The Law of Consequence — The City That Kept Both Scars
*Volume X · foundational Reality Consequence Engine / first playable Crossings slice · 11 October 2026*

The Unmade can alter the shape of a street, still a bell for a heartbeat, or coax a bridge from its surviving witnesses. But streets are walked upon. Bells have keepers. A bridge may bring a refugee to safety and also bring a tax collector to the door. **The world must change with the alteration, rather than waiting politely for the spell effect to disappear.**

This is *The Law of Consequence*: each legitimate reality action has a mechanically traceable life across architecture, movement, combat, individual witnesses and the city's durable evidence. Neither a universal reputation number nor a single cosmic interpretation can replace those different effects. A character's experience can contradict the city's public response while remaining entirely sincere.

### The crossing and its three transient architectures

The initial gameplay stage takes place within a bounded part of **the Crossings**, not all nine realms simultaneously. A rite cast nearby may briefly make a safe span accessible, strain the passage with a dangerous mass, or give a threshold two visibly incompatible arrangements. The same casting event also modifies how nearby hostile stalkers and watchers close distance. In the prototype the masonry is graybox geometry: a real actor with collision, not a completed animation or architectural art asset.

**The supported architecture outcomes are:**

1. **Safe span:** witness-supported and protective rites produce a temporary physical approach; enemies approach more slowly, creating a legible chance to reposition without freezing them.
2. **Strained span:** an unbound law or borrowed tomorrow distorts the approach; enemies move more aggressively and the changed physical structure demands attention.
3. **Double threshold:** a borrowed life or paradox briefly gives the crossing two incompatible built shapes; enemies react with a different pursuit tempo, and the player can use geometry rather than a hidden numerical morality advantage.

A temporary structure expires on the saved **world clock**, not when the camera leaves the region. Leaving and returning does not grant a duplicate ritual. The rite's own strain and cooldown remain governed by the established Tenfold Chronicle; no new arbitrary energy or free power system is invented here.

### Ten disciplines, ten reasons the city responds

| Rite already in canon | Temporary environment | Combat rhythm near the Crossings | Human consequence reading |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Unwrite Law | Strained span | Enemies close faster | What supports a house when its laws fail? |
| Witnesscraft | Safe span | Enemies close more cautiously | Whose independent memory may hold the road? |
| Borrowed Lives | Double threshold | Enemy pace increases | Who is responsible for work done by a possible other self? |
| Legacy Forging | Safe span | Approach slows | Can ordinary labor be remembered without becoming compulsory service? |
| Living Roads | Safe span | Approach slows | Who may use a road owned by neither community? |
| Tomorrow's Debt | Strained span | Approach accelerates | Who will pay when tomorrow's borrowed strength is gone? |
| Understanding Bosses | Safe span | Enemies withdraw/approach more slowly | Can mercy leave both victim and witness intact? |
| Paradox Convergence | Double threshold | Approach quickens | What if both contradictory addresses remain occupied? |
| Oathbinding | Safe span | Approach slows | Can a public promise protect a nameless guest? |
| Unreliable Cartography | Safe span | Approach slows modestly | Who is harmed when an uncertain route is printed as certain? |

The table describes source-defined local profiles, **not ten fully animated and realm-specific spell manifestations**. Temporary combat tuning is the enemy's actual swept movement speed, not invented damage calculations or invisible AI strategy. Most rites already have distinct direct abilities elsewhere (for example, Unwrite Law's chosen gravity, sound, or momentum). The consequence engine connects those abilities to the same local world record.

### A civic act cannot happen without witnesses

Hessa would never describe an unobserved reality rite as something she saw. Sevrin would not date a structure that he never examined. Orrel will not infer an entire city agrees with him merely because the player cast a powerful spell twice.

A single cast can alter the temporary masonry even if there are no human onlookers. **Permanent public traces require a mastered rite actually witnessed at the location.** Two distinct qualifying rites on the sheltering side produce a **Shared Passage** record. Two distinct qualifying rites on the interrogating side produce a **Public Inquiry** record. They aren't a score that can be ground by repeating the same spell.

Crucially, if the city has honestly witnessed at least two different examples *of both kinds*, it keeps **both records**: the protective way and the public question. This outcome is called the **Contested Accord**. Neither side of the contradiction is magically erased. One can walk beside a stone supporting access and another requesting structural investigation; a responsible civilization might need both.

No character is told a global event by telepathy. Residents within sight of the actual caster acquire a personal memory. Nearby others can later learn an attributed rumor from that witness through the existing physically bounded conversation rules. They must distinguish *“I saw a crossing change”* from *“I heard that it changed”*. Their trust and fear respond differently to a witnessed protective action versus a witnessed unsettling inquiry; this never silently replaces their authored biography or private concerns.

### The two costs of the same passage

The ordinary market bell rings. A player opens a safer approach with Witnesscraft, and a trader finishes carrying flour that would otherwise have been delayed. The player sees a temporary span and a changed combat lane. A resident who actually witnessed the action remembers its practical help. Her gratitude does **not** prove the bridge's history.

Days later another witness sees Unwrite Law expose the uncertainty beneath the same structure. The investigation gains a defensible public question. When both kinds of testimony accumulate, the city has to hold relief and caution together. For a future cinematic, the sound must start with street life: wooden wheels, worn shoes and the argument of builders. There should be no generic blast of blue light that obscures whether an actual person was helped.

**Orrel:** “It stands long enough for people to cross. That is a fact. It still has to be someone's work.”

**Hessa:** “A fact that makes the road safer is not the same thing as permission to count everyone on it.”

**Sevrin:** “Then show me how the stone was supported. Show me the witness. Tell me which part you know.”

The lines are *authored future scene writing*, not a claim that these specific voice-acted conversations have been imported into Unreal. The in-game source now produces separate direct/heard response text in resident conversations.

### What the engine must never fake

The Crossings model is the **first representative implementation** of a larger system. It provides ten mapped rite profiles, world-clock temporary architecture, tactical changes to actual local enemy movement, physically witnessed resident social reactions, and save-backed public civic structures. It retains the established Tenfold Rite costs/cooldowns and the player’s existing authored faction choices. Its record rejects invalid rite IDs, incompatible witness masks, non-finite durations, contradictory save snapshots and a civic history with an impossible witness type. On failed writes, the new consequence state is rolled back instead of displayed as a completed civic event.

This model does not yet provide physically accurate falling masonry, authored structural engineering puzzles, a complete evacuation simulation, an advanced tactical combat AI, long-term NPC employment changes, production art, unique environmental soundscapes, or bespoke rules in every realm. All remain distinct future deliverables requiring proper assets and Unreal Editor testing. The current Source CI is a portable domain/source check; it is **not** certification of a compiled Windows UE game.

### The nine-realm expansion rule

Every future civilization must receive the same **causal accounting**, not the same civic morality or identical architecture:

- **Bellwold:** count fires, winter shelter beds and protected identities separately. Protection and public recognition can conflict.
- **Paperhaven:** testimony has a source, consent boundary and disputed chronology. An archive can publish an objection without publishing its vulnerable author.
- **Saltwake:** a dry harbor's tariffs and stormwater routes turn spatial magic into an economic and engineering question.
- **Cinderhold:** heat, fuel and communal kilns mean a spell has material beneficiaries, labor costs and dangerous winter trade-offs.
- **Orravane:** sound maintains suspended homes; a temporary silencing rite needs evacuation planning, not only a damage number.
- **Drevlach:** a changed address, ancestral labor and the continuity of occupied structures must remain connected.
- **Other established realms:** their distinct language, law, physical hazards and historical debts determine consequences, never generic biome reskins.

The climax with Nhal-Vey can then test the player's literacy in trade-offs: they must protect lives while confronting incompatible accounts, without acquiring one omnipotent button that makes everyone else agree.

The unresolved question still rattles Orrel's nail. The world no longer pretends the nail is alone.

## 22. The Nine-Realm Reaction System — One Rite, Nine Consequences
*Volume X · evolving franchise and game design Bible · 11 October 2026*

The greatest error in any map of The Unmade is to assume the same spell is the same *event* everywhere. Stone, water, sound, winter heat, the ownership of time, missing names, and unfinished laws are not interchangeable materials. Each civilization must deal with what a change physically threatens, what it costs to repair, who is allowed to document it, and who actually witnessed it.

The **Nine-Realm Reaction System** extends the first local Crossings model with nine different policies over the world’s real atlas coordinates. It preserves the existing Reach ledger so the player's older choices remain true. Eight additional civic histories—one per outer realm—record what each community can justify with its own witnesses. None inherits evidence from a distant city merely because the hero has visited both.

### 22.1 The physical distinction

Every realm uses the existing ten learned Tenfold rites. The original strain and cooldown rules remain the price paid for a cast. In a world where the cast is locally supported, it manifests as one of three temporary physical graybox forms—safe span, strained span, or contradictory threshold—yet the *same* form can have a different hazard and combat meaning.

Consider Living Roads:
- In **Saltwake**, the wardens see a possible passage to a shared rain cistern. The temporary road lessens storm exposure, but does not invent fresh water. Net-menders and water brokers may disagree over what maintaining the channel costs.
- In **Cinderhold**, the temporary route is a distribution line for community embers. A safer path lessens the immediate burden of heat surges, while the assembly still has to decide which household supplies the fuel.
- In **Orravane**, the same passage depends on song as structural support. The safer path dampens the strain of a falling note rather than changing the physical idea of rain or coal.

The danger system consumes actual realm-specific percentages for the current hazard pulse, distinguishing physical injury from reality strain. Nearby enemies also consume each realm's different movement multiplier: a Stalker in the Inverted Choir should not move as though standing on an ordinary dry road. The source implementation does *not* claim full fluid dynamics, acoustics, or bespoke boss tactical AI.

### 22.2 Nine cultures, their materials, and their laws

| Realm and recognizable settlement | What is actually at risk | Institution and standing civic question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Threefold Reach / the Crossings** | Contracted stone, builders' wages and safe refuge passage | Bridge Ledger Court: whose survey defines a road, and who paid to build it? |
| **Widowed Rain / Saltwake** | Counted stormwater, dry-harbor tolls and the shared cistern | Rainward tally: who sold the same storm twice? |
| **Hearth Beneath / Cinderhold** | Communal embers, winter heat and shared kilns | Hearth assembly: can a family keep warm without owning the flame? |
| **Tidal Ledger / Drevlach** | Collateral, ferry dues and tomorrow's indebted passage | Debt adjudicators: can a future wage be seized for a journey that never happened? |
| **Sky Below / Orravane** | Load-bearing song, counterweights and inhabited suspended roofs | Inverted choir: when one verse is erased, which families must be evacuated? |
| **Cinder Spine / Vathless** | Quarry seams, wages, displaced builders and family memory | Quarry council: who is responsible when the mine works an ancestry out of the record? |
| **Hundred Unlived / Eillun** | Anonymous shelter, unregistered doors and consent | Consent census: may an impossible citizen be named without permission? |
| **Orchard of Kings / Tharniv** | Communal grafts, inheritance and roots that carry unwritten law | Untitled court: who claims ownership of a road that grew before the crown existed? |
| **First Absence / Auvren** | Stable memories, competing origins and usable geometry | First witnesses: how can a passage be trusted when both maps are valid locally? |

These are authoritative realm policies. They provide distinct local environmental effects and the words attached to real civic evidence. **The full producer-consumer simulation for water, fuel, wages, collateral and roofs is future engineering**. Their named dependencies are defined now precisely so future economic transactions, evacuation scenes and quests can implement them without reinventing the societies.

### 22.3 Personal history resists a shared story

A resident does not receive a scene's knowledge from a realm-wide global integer. An actual cast must happen inside the correct local world focus. A nearby stable NPC with a clear line of sight can receive the cast as a personal observation. Existing, bounded conversation may later pass the report as hearsay with the original speaker's stable identity. The report carries its **realm source**, so a witness to Saltwake's storm cannot be mistaken for a witness to Cinderhold's kiln.

Named examples:

**Thenn of Saltwake** may hear the rain counted and challenge a broker with the tally. His skepticism is local: it should not make him immediately know how Orrel's bridge was built.

**Rheva of Cinderhold** may welcome a safe ember channel but still ask which household bears the night watch. She has been guarding the flame since before the protagonist arrived.

**The Choir Master of Orravane** can interpret a changed harmonic span as a support warning without naming a hidden singer. A firsthand verse is different from hearing another choir member describe it.

**The Unlived Census Keeper of Eillun** may insist on consent before opening a registration door. Creating a passage is not permission to register every traveler.

These are attributed character points of view. A live character may contradict a local civic record while retaining their own relationships and identity. Player dialogue must continue to distinguish “I saw” from “someone told me.” The stable resident ID is not discarded when moving between scenes.

### 22.4 Civics that can disagree without resetting each other

The Crossings retains its preexisting, independent civic record. The eight outer realms now possess additional save-backed records. In each, the author’s familiar evidence rule is applied independently: two **different**, mastered, locally witnessed caring rites can create a protected public passage; two different interrogating rites can create a public inquiry. When both forms of testimony exist, both civic markers must survive side by side: the Contested Accord.

Nothing about a care record makes its citizens universally benevolent. A shared safe passage can still be expensive. An inquiry can be brave or cruel depending on the subsequent institutions. The engine records evidence that a decision is publicly supportable, not the best or only moral answer.

There are new source-spawned tangible temporary forms and two persistent marker positions for each outer realm. The same single world clock determines expiry; there are no hidden long-term buffs when moving to a new realm and no remote automatic civic consent.

### 22.5 A lived gameplay journey

The traveler enters Saltwake during a storm pulse. The player's acquired rite briefly changes a shelter approach, and the injury from the active local weather pressure adjusts. The player can see the change and feel a different tactical pursuit from an enemy. A navigator who genuinely saw the transformation acquires a firsthand memory; the rain keeper hears the story later only if someone can pass it on.

The next passage to Cinderhold has no right to borrow Saltwake's proof. The traveler may cast the same learned rite there, but the heat pressure and enemy movement are different, and Rheva's civic assembly has its own memory. The player may earn enough firsthand testimony to support a common ember channel. In Orravane, a falling note can increase *strain* instead of damaging flesh; one spectacular cast cannot satisfy the choir's two-distinct-rite requirement.

What ties these moments together is not a palette swap: a player needs to learn how a city's dependencies alter the same skill, choose appropriate counterplay, and respect what witnesses actually know. The sequel or television adaptation can return to those same people in later years without replacing them with anonymous exposition.

### 22.6 Source implementation, quality limits and next acts

**Implemented at this source milestone:**
- Nine canon-aligned region policies tied to their existing atlas coordinates and actual local cast positions.
- Preserved v1 Crossings history plus eight completely separated, optional schema-1 save records, with atomic restoration and prevention of corrupted-data overwrites.
- A locally active three-form architectural world prop per outer realm, and independent physically represented care/inquiry/contested civic records.
- Real local enemy pursuit speed variation and pressure changes applied to the game’s existing storm, ember, tide, quarry, census, roots and unmooring hazard pulses, including both actual health injury and reality strain.
- Named, local, direct-versus-heard NPC reactions with source-identity isolation. No remote reputation teleportation.
- In the existing Crossings market, a small temporary supply/trust adjustment is passed to the actual bounded purchase-price rule; other realms' proposed economic dependencies do not yet pretend to be running full trading systems.
- A world journal capable of describing the current realm’s actual materials, institutions and civic position, with native source and pipeline tests.

**Still to engineer and playtest:** real authored walkable meshes instead of block proxies, rooted plants and physics-supported bridges, self-contained sound simulation, dynamic navigation/cover AI, real resource flows and trade in eight outer realms, individual household and employment impacts, full voice performances, controller-compatible evidence UI, low-vision access, full game balance and Windows Unreal Editor/PIE verification.

The next design horizon is **Local Material Consequence**: turn each realm dependency into an accountable supply and labor system that a player can witness, repair, exploit or safeguard, with actual inventory movements, transaction logs and returning resident scenes. The final confrontation with Nhal-Vey should test what the player has learned about the incompatibility of simultaneous truths, not simply unlock a button that erases history.

*A road made possible by one spell should never make the lives beside it vanish.*

## 23. Local Material Consequences — What the Miracle Costs Tomorrow
*Volume X · deterministic v3 material-and-labor gameplay foundation · 11 October 2026*

The first time a traveler watches Cinderhold's shared ember light a whole hall, the flame looks like a miracle. The next morning, Rheva counts the baskets. The children are still cold. Somebody has to carry fuel from the common store, somebody must tend the kiln, and the half-collapsed stone flue will not mend itself because the protagonist has learned an elegant reality rite.

**The new governing rule of The Unmade is that a resource must exist, be produced or be physically transported before anyone is credited with receiving it.** Each society has specific dependencies and finite units. Its people have work to do even when the player is exploring an entirely different realm.

### 23.1 Nine material identities

| Settlement | Finite stored unit | Labor and household obligation | Damage and recovery |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Crossings | Bracing fittings | Bridge carpenters supply safe-passage homes | Repairs consume real bridge fittings; protected passage does not cancel owed wages |

These stocks are **different local goods**; one realm's cask cannot magically appear as another realm's basket. Every civilization begins with a small, explicitly defined store and household reserve. Its daily workers produce a bounded number of units, then ordinary local haulage moves units to the homes. Households consume a defined daily need. The warehouse, households and repairs are separate accounts.

### 23.2 The second dawn

Cinderhold's common-store keeper writes a number next to each dwelling. A workday has passed, and the store has been replenished by a finite shift of kiln tenders. Households need seven ember baskets each day. A courier can usually move five from the store to the heated homes. Without intervention, the shortage grows and the integrity of the district's shared structures falls. More baskets in the warehouse do not heat a home that never receives them.

The nine towns advance **once per in-game day using the existing twenty-minute world clock**, not once per screen refresh. Work and household consumption can proceed while the player travels elsewhere, but there is no offline-money faucet or accelerated speculative catch-up for old saves. At night the named residents still retain their own homes and personal schedules.

Once the city suffers shortages, existing named couriers and merchants turn toward actual material depots during daytime routines; other available roles respond at homes. When buildings are damaged, guards or wanderers assigned to that place can turn toward the repair location. Immediate danger and actual individual incident responses still take priority. This is an authored schedule-level work shift, not a claim that every named worker already has detailed wage contracts and animated hands.

### 23.3 A player can help, but the goods must move

There are three material interaction stations per realm: a **stockroom**, a **household relief point**, and a **repair site**. Each is a physical graybox actor with a stable tag. The familiar E key is used inside a small reach radius with an unobstructed sightline, respecting the priority of an even closer resident.

At the stockroom, the protagonist may withdraw **one five-unit relief pack** if that specific city has at least five units to spare. Stock falls immediately and custody goes into the existing SaveGame. The player must carry it physically to the same realm's household relief point. A carried packet from Saltwake cannot be turned into Cinderhold embers; no second packet may be drawn until the first is honestly delivered. On real delivery, exactly five units appear in the household reserve, the delivery counter increases once, and the held packet disappears.

The repair site requires a genuinely damaged structure and enough stored material to pay its **distinct local repair cost**. A successful repair spends those materials and increases structural integrity by eighteen, capped at one hundred; attempting to repair intact masonry earns neither a free resource nor a hidden experience prize. Save failures roll back the changed stock, carried custody, or building integrity before the world confirms completion.

Visible damage, low household reserve and repaired structures are represented by separate, condition-dependent graybox actors. A returning player can see both the support that was repaired and any newly developed shortage. The journal lists the day's store, household reserves, need, available workers, structural condition, deliveries, repairs and carried packet.

### 23.4 The labor of a witnessed reality

Two different mastered and locally witnessed protective rites already contribute to a city's civic public response. The material system uses that *existing proven event* to make one additional worker available to the daily production shift in that realm. It does not materialize goods on cast. The extra worker's output enters the store on the next actual workday and still has to be hauled to homes.

A contradictory civic inquiry does not simply turn into a moral penalty. Witnesses can demand a public investigation while production continues. When both public claims coexist, the material ledger still records concrete labor, goods and unmet household need rather than an invented happiness score. A structure can be repaired and remain politically disputed.

A low structural integrity can reduce the effective number of workers available; shortages further erode structural integrity. A player may have to decide whether to divert finite resources to urgent household warmth or to pay for a repair that makes future labor safer. This is an emerging gameplay conflict rather than a scripted fake choice.

### 23.5 Recognizable inhabitants, not generic labor nodes

**Rheva, Ember Warden:** *“That basket is not a prayer. Somebody carried it here. I can account for every one.”* She recognizes an actual shortage from the communal public supply board, without suddenly knowing what happened to an unrelated city.

**Sella, Navigator of Saltwake:** *“Count the water before you call the new road a rescue. The storm does not promise to stop tomorrow.”* Her weather observations are distinct from Orrel's accounting of bridge fittings.

**The Choir Master of Orravane:** *“A note holds a roof only if someone sings it. Ask the people beneath it how long they can wait.”* The city consumes resonant supports, not magically rebranded rain.

**Orrel:** *“An open bridge and a mended bridge are two different facts. The carpenter who worked here should be remembered for both.”*

These dramatic lines define future authored performance scenes and character voice. The present UE source uses public shortage boards and preserved individual biographies, not these fully voiced cutscenes. A player's labor and civic deeds cannot declare a protected resident's name public.

## 24. The Living Economy — Nine Households, Two Roads, and the Weight of a Receipt
*Volume X · deterministic v4 livelihood and trade vertical slice · 11 October 2026*

It is easy to speak about the heat of Cinderhold as though heat were its own master. It is more difficult to ask Bes, the coal runner, whether his wages reached the home he shares. The fire may be communal. The baskets are finite. Bes is neither an interchangeable worker count nor a number on a distant reputation meter.

**The Living Economy** gives an existing named resident in each of the nine realms a traceable livelihood: a job, an identifiable home, a finite employer treasury, earned wages, and unpaid obligations. It retains the aggregate v3 workforce for other residents; this is a first *named* accounting layer, not the false claim that every adult of every realm already has a detailed employment contract.

### The owners of work, and the debts that remember it

Kesta's rented room in the Crossings is attached to her work as a porter. Mera works the Saltwake tide route. Bes carries Cinderhold's ember baskets. Orshel maintains Drevlach's dock. Vhaurn keeps Orravane's supports, Torrun maintains a quarry dwelling in Vathless, Auvet builds refuge in Eillun without registering their guests, Imro tends an unclaimed communal plot in Tharniv, and Thae keeps Auvren's threshold.

Each worker's existing, stable identity is used. A wage of two local marks is **transferred** from an initial municipal treasury of thirty-two to the named worker's own earned account for each actually simulated shift. If the employer cannot pay, the earned account is not secretly credited. The missed two marks enter a separate *owed* register. A later payment can discharge an actual debt only from available municipal funds. Money is conserved across all nine employers, their workers and any explicitly reserved trade escrow; the engine may not create marks merely because the player returned.

An owned wage is not a grant of ownership over the common kiln, the dock or the town's warehouse. The v3 stockroom and communal household supplies remain separately counted. This division protects both wages and shared goods from double counting.

### A shipment has two towns and a real destination

Two reciprocal contracts are introduced as a first international-trade route, not a universal teleporting trade system.

**Saltwake to Cinderhold:** Mera's town can release five *sealed rainwater casks* to Cinderhold, where the casks support kiln cooling and safe drinking. The five casks must first be withdrawn from Saltwake's own warehouse, and Cinderhold commits six marks from its finite treasury into escrow. The player physically transports the signed cargo to the separate Cinderhold receiving station. Only at an actual E receipt are the casks credited as **foreign casks** and the escrow released to the source employer. Rainwater never magically becomes ember baskets.

**Cinderhold to Saltwake:** Bes's town can similarly release five *common ember baskets*. Saltwake reserves six marks and the player brings those exact baskets to a separate receiving station. Those baskets become an **imported heating reserve**, not local rainwater.

A single inter-realm consignment may be in custody at a time. It remains carried across game days and saves. The source and destination are both grounded in the atlas, and proximity and line of sight are required at the physical stations. A wrong-city receipt, duplicate collection, insufficient source stock or unreserved payment cannot succeed. Ordinary v3 relief packs cannot silently substitute for these foreign goods.

### What happens after delivery

When a shortage actually occurs in the destination city, a unit of received foreign support can be consumed to reduce damage to occupied infrastructure. The goods' foreign identity remains recorded in their route ledger. This helps the destination survive without pretending a bucket of Saltwake water replaces five baskets of Cinderhold fuel or that a shipment produces its own salary.

The destination's named worker can remember the received contract. Mera or Bes is able to say how much pay is genuinely theirs, what the employer still owes, and how many delivered manifests they have witnessed. Their earlier biographies, return encounters and ordinary movement have not been replaced by generic economy-generated characters.

This work is morally complicated. A city can protect its shelter with a real import while failing to pay a local worker. An authorized contract can save lives while revealing the fragility of a municipal treasury. A familiar visitor may discover that a beloved resident has been owed wages for many days. None of these states is a global Good Person score.

### The earned contradiction at Nhal-Vey's mask

The first defeat of Nhal-Vey reveals that victory was incomplete. If, and **only if**, both reciprocal contracts were physically delivered and saved, that reveal can also acknowledge the documented mutual aid between Saltwake and Cinderhold. Nhal-Vey's claim that incompatible worlds cannot carry one another is challenged by actual receipts, employer payments and human memory.

This is **earned counter-testimony**, not a secret instant-win ability. The existing boss attack sequence, late-game choices, returning echo and unresolved mysteries are preserved. The new line is a connective gameplay reward grounded in earlier civilian action, not a substitute for completing the confrontation.

### The next character-driven chapter

**The Ninth Paybook** should follow a worker who is indispensable yet unpaid. A coming storm forces Saltwake to choose between funding a new shipment, repaying overdue wages and reinforcing homes; Mera refuses to become a free instrument for a player's convenient heroic ending. A genuinely branching quest must allow negotiation, postponement, physical carriage, recovery after failed deals and later consequences.

**The Ember Claim** should give Bes a specific home and a human relationship beyond the current aggregate housing reserve. A complete version would include exact wages, days missed, family obligations, different delivery permissions, and a daily schedule physically tied to his counted output.

**The Two Receipts** should become a voiced late-game conversation in which Bes and Mera can disagree about what the trade meant, without either needing to know the other's private thoughts. Their source-identified histories ought to shape the music, blocking, dialogue and the visual memory of the world.

### Source implementation and honest boundary

**Implemented in this source milestone:** nine canon-stable worker identities with named homes, employer and worker-owned currency ledgers, finite paid shifts, tracked arrears, conserved transaction balances, two escorted physical reciprocal cargo contracts, destination escrow, distinct foreign support inventory, a shortage-dependent consumption benefit, the world-clock save loop, a local trade journal, return dialogue for the nine workers, and an optional receipt-qualified Nhal-Vey mask line. Additional version-one-compatible SaveGame fields and native/source tests reject forged deliveries, impossible balances and corrupted cargo.

**Not yet built or verified:** separately simulated tenant inventories for every resident, comprehensive wage contracts across all NPCs, full route navigation/escort AI, wide-ranging commercial prices, diplomatic taxes, player wage income, household cooking and heating animation, final recorded voices and subtitles, authored cinematics, complex production buildings, Windows Unreal Editor/UHT/PIE compilation, full-world latency and collision QA. The two corridors are a first operational slice, not a fully global transport economy.

*Someone who keeps the world warm must also be allowed to come home.*

## 25. Lives Behind the Ledger — Bes and the Hearth That Cannot Be Forgiven
*Volume X · Character and consequence chapter · 11 October 2026*

Cinderhold has a communal flame, and that is the first misunderstanding. The heat does not carry itself. Bes carries it. He has a door, a floor marked with ash, a name in the work ledger and a place he returns to when the baskets become too heavy. The city counts his work in full and his pay only when money exists. The reader should remember him for the tone of his voice when he realizes those numbers have stopped agreeing.

### A house before a heroic choice

The coal runner's home is not an unlockable illustration or a quest-stage flag presented as architecture. In the blockout it is a collidable, visitable house beside his Cinderhold route. Its small hearth possesses a **separate, finite reserve of warming baskets**, not an unexplained copy of the communal warehouse. A workday consumes one unit of household warmth. The traveler encounters the problem only after the existing municipality has actually missed Bes's wages. Initially, speaking to him is ordinary conversation; no fabricated debt unlocks the investigation. Once the unpaid count is real, Bes can state what was taken from his time. The player then **visits his home** and has a reason to know whom the work affects. Only after that inspection may Rheva, Cinderhold's named ember warden, corroborate the municipal ledger. Her corroboration is not mind reading: she reads a local public wage account as the person charged with defending it. Ovenna, the existing warmth keeper, knows the home's cold or warmth without pretending to have attended a negotiation she missed.

**Bes, Coal Runner.** He knows the weight of the basket and the day he was not paid. Menus must show outcomes in complete, non-moralizing language, including **what is *not* solved**. Relief: “five baskets go to this hearth; municipal pay remains owed.” Walkout: “Bes refuses a shift; Cinderhold produces less tomorrow.” Restitution: “actual town marks transfer to Bes and Mera.” Mera's guarantee: “her money moves and the town still owes her.” No approval score or fabricated permanent happy ending.

### 26.6 Interaction and subtitle safety

A cinema-grade layout must keep the actor, props, and physical world visible while the text remains readable on a phone-sized screen and 16:9 display. The black bar is not a place to hide gameplay. A lower-third subtitle region should have a strong contrast backdrop when needed, an adjustable scale, safe insets, no art cropping, and no more than one authored beat at a time. **Never overlay two chapters' dialogue into a single static panel or cut a scene illustration to force the text on screen.**

The authored presentation bridge emits timed Blueprint multicast events containing speaker ID, actual subtitle, shot cue, Foley cue, blocking and voice direction. It has a skip/clear function and a native readable caption fallback. The bridge does **not** invent a finished voice asset, create a Sequencer track, bake an animation, or synthesize convincing Foley by naming a file. Those are explicit Unreal production tasks. Captions are available even when the future voice component is disabled or a localization fails.

Accessibility and controller requirements: distinct prompts for Inspect versus Commit, second-press confirmation that works on gamepads, optional auto-advance with enough reading time, adjustable subtitle background and scale, complete sound captions where needed, camera-shake reduction, no interaction based only on color, and motion-reduced camera transitions. Skipping a scene never skips the underlying source-authoritative wage transaction.

### 26.7 Returning to the familiar

An NPC must be able to recognize that the person speaking to them has returned. On an eligible later encounter, Bes or Mera can run a short, throttled reprise of the same interior or dock framing rather than the initial exposition. The game engine derives the numeric balances from the real saved record, and the authored return beat supplements them. No scene plays for a resident who has never reported a wage dispute. The return scene is rate-limited; a player should not be punished with identical speeches on every rapid interaction.

The best reaction is sometimes an altered routine rather than another paragraph: Bes still walks home during refusal, and the worksite has a discernible empty place. The first time the player visits his house during the night, it should feel different from the same room after restitution. The heat returns because real goods were delivered, not because the scene camera decided the player chose the benevolent ending.

### 26.8 Music and silence as a recurring argument

The production motif consists of four layers: a slow mechanical beat from shared kiln work; sparse ember movement at the hearth; Saltwake's irregular percussive water; and a near-silent human breath. In the ordinary city these layers coexist imperfectly. After the strike, the missing kiln beat must be *subtracted*, not replaced with melodramatic strings. If Mera underwrites Bes, let Saltwake's water enter Cinderhold's soundtrack as a dissonant, brief response, but never as a magical claim that a water cask has turned into fuel.

At Nhal-Vey's first mask-break, reuse the exact earlier physical sound: falling basket, metal marks, or sealed paper depending on the saved branch. Three memories can then be distinct to the ear **before** exposition explains them. The boss's existing soundtrack, combat phases and unsolved lore must survive this callback. The missing sound of Chapter One remains a separate unresolved cosmological mystery.

### 26.9 What is implemented and what is still demanded

**Current source milestone:** complete 39-beat deterministic screenplay matrix with explicit actor/camera/Foley/VO direction; timed, skippable scene event bridge; backward-compatible text subtitle fallback; informed double-interaction commitment at the physical stations; an actually enterable multi-part home blockout with work-life props; distinct later Bes/Mera scene triggers; authored optional mask-break return; CI contracts and updated cumulative canon.

**Not yet claimed:** recorded professional dialogue, production Foley WAV files, mixed final soundtrack, automatic Sequencer generation, real 3D skeletal actor animation, finished detailed interior art, actual visually polished UMG choice widget, Unreal Engine Windows UHT/PIE acceptance, controller/device camera testing, or complete AAA film quality. This chapter provides precise scene authoring and source-backed playback hooks so those can be made without inventing the quest from scratch on the first PC.

*The quiet choice must have the weight of a battle, and the battle must remember the quiet choice.*

## 27. The Audible Shift — The Sound a Household Keeps
*Volume X · production-grade sound identity and accessible dialogue delivery contract · 11 October 2026*

Cinderhold has never been silent. There are bellows, grit beneath boots, baskets dragged across stone and the distant metallic disagreement of communal marks being counted. Bes's story changes that soundscape not by adding a grand theme, but by removing the weight-bearing rhythm he supplied. The absence of one worker is something a city should hear.

The sound of **The Unpaid Shift** now has an executable prototype layer. Thirty-nine existing authored beats reference thirty-five individually named Foley cues. Each has a reproducibly generated, original stereo PCM WAV with validated amplitude and a unique recorded SHA-256 digest. These are diegetic *placeholder effects*, not human speech, not claims of award-winning Foley, and not the game's final theatrical mix. They provide working acoustic timing and a consistent library so the first Windows Unreal import does not begin with silence.

### 27.1 Listening as material evidence

**Basket / ash:** A grainy, hollow drop signals a coal basket being set down. It occurs after actual labor rather than as an arbitrary reward sound. Ash footsteps, rattle, stacking and settling are distinct, though currently synthesized from textures rather than field-recorded coal and boots.

**Iron / coin / wages:** Counted metal marks sound crisp and short. A refusal cannot suddenly sound like a triumphant payday. If the municipal ledger cannot actually pay both Bes and a guarantor, no paid-receipt sequence should be allowed to play.

**Paper / consent:** Mera's pledge makes a dry, salt-stiff paper noise. It is quieter than municipal stamping because it is her owned savings, not a public transaction the story should celebrate. A successful promise to Bes that leaves Mera unpaid must retain the paper motif in later returns and in Nhal-Vey's first mask revelation.

**Fire / home:** A warm room and a cold room retain the same address and belongings. A gentle ember catch or fading flame is a supporting sound, never the exclusive communication channel: captions and visible saved household heat must still make the state legible with sound muted.

**Missing shift:** The work rhythm thins out rather than being replaced by an unearned victory fanfare. The absence must remain audible within Cinderhold's wider city ambience. The code has an original synthetic `kiln_missing_rhythm` cue for prototype timing, with final spatial mix and actual worker animation still to author.

**Nhal-Vey:** The soundtrack should refer to the very metal, basket or paper the player encountered earlier. The final opponent does not narrate an instant victory. A sound recalls a recorded life; the fight continues.

### 27.2 The first non-clipping caption contract

The existing timed scene bridge now has a native Unreal UMG caption fallback. Each beat displays its own authored speaker and subtitle in a lower-third region anchored to safe horizontal margins, with wrapping and a restrained dark backdrop. It does not cover the middle of the scene with a long chapter card. It does not steal the player's controls; the screen remains interactive and skip reliably clears the overlay.

This approach is practical rather than decorative: a player who cannot hear, or whose imported SoundWave is missing, receives the exact same dialogue text at the same beat boundaries. A spoken actor's identity is distinct from stage narration. Bes, Rheva, Ovenna and Mera remain recognizable in text even when an optional Foley clip cannot load.

The game should eventually offer subtitle scale presets, contrast levels, separate sound-effect descriptions and on-screen speaker placement appropriate to every supported display. The current native source creates one readable lower-third overlay and timed text; it is *not yet* a polished, audited final localization and accessibility system.

### 27.3 Deterministic audio build and truthful voice boundary

The project's source contains `Scripts/build_unpaid_shift_audio.py`: a deterministic, standard-library-only audio generator. From the canonical 39-beat screenplay header, it exports thirty-five real, independently named, 32-kHz, 16-bit, stereo `SFX_US_*.wav` clips plus a JSON manifest containing sound names, rates, durations, peak/RMS information and SHA-256 digests. The build refuses zero-amplitude or clipping output and verifies the set of expected cues.

`Scripts/unreal_editor/import_unpaid_shift_audio.py` verifies every original WAV before importing. It does not overwrite custom professional SoundWave assets, altered Foley, or separately recorded voices. The Windows quickstart generates the pack, dry-checks the manifest, and—when the authorized Unreal Editor staging phase is enabled—imports the thirty-five named SoundWaves and writes a receipt. Runtime playback attempts only the cue that the current beat actually references; if its file is not present, the subtitled scene still plays.

This is the first working acoustic path for this quest, **not narration**. The project must never describe the source's `voice` direction column as a recorded human line. The actual performances for Bes, Rheva, Ovenna, Mera, the environment and Nhal-Vey require direction, recording, editing, localization, mixing, lip synchronization and device-level tests. The existing narrator assembly kit is for the broader trailer and does not suddenly become a new professional cast.

### 27.4 Living scenes need living people

A warmer hearth still requires actual coal baskets, and a returning Bes still requires his own accumulated cold days, wages and work refusal. Audio cannot create these truths. It responds to them. The highest-order invariant is that the save decides whether a production loss, transfer, consent or return happened; a soundtrack or cutscene may illustrate that event only after a successful authoritative transaction.

The next production wave must replace non-animated blockout performances with rigged characters, motion-blocking and expressive gestures; construct the home's finished stone, bedding, ledger, threshold and work props; author readable interaction cards with explicit per-branch costs; and drive controlled cinematic cameras without losing the player's spatial freedom. A working source caption widget is not the same thing as a finished Unreal Sequencer shot.

### 27.5 Technical status and continuing promises

**Now implemented in source:** generated original 35-effect stereo audio library from 39 named beat cues; exact-asset runtime sound lookup with graceful no-sound behavior; one-at-a-time safe-area native Unreal UMG captions with speaker identification and guaranteed skip clear; source-level build/module dependencies; a guarded first-PC Unreal SoundWave importer with no unintended overwrites; Windows quickstart and CI checks. The v5–v6 household, dialogue, physical choices and saved Nhal-Vey paths remain untouched.

**Still to certify:** actual UE 5.8 UHT compile, imported audio listening in PIE, loudness matching against gameplay audio, spatialized environmental Foley, full staged animations, detailed home materials, professional dialogue, genuine narration, subtitle accessibility testing, cinematics with proper camera blocking, and a complete player-tested quest. Original generated placeholders intentionally sound like prototypes; they must later be replaced without changing the canonical timeline or gameplay ledger.

*A lost wage can be an absent sound. It cannot be an absent person.*

# THE UNMADE — VOLUME X, CHAPTER 30

## THE BODY THAT REMEMBERS: PERFORMANCE AND PERIL

**Playable World v10 · October 11, 2026 · After source revision v9 · Actual asset kit and Unreal source, pending engine acceptance**

> The first honest movement of a frightened person is not an attack. It is the instant they decide whether to stand their ground.

### 30.1 A world is not alive because everything moves

A city requires movement with cause: a worker must be able to stop their shift without becoming a broken animation, a witness must turn to face the person they address, and a creature must reveal its intent before damage arrives. The Unmade has long promised societies with their own account of reality's fractures; these new performances must make that distinction visible instead of substituting motion for identity. Bes's refusal to work is a choice with a saved production cost, not a generic idle state. Rheva's acknowledgment of his unpaid wages must feel like the admission of a person who risks being remembered for it. Mera cannot be posed as an automatic benefactor: the camera can show her consenting only after the authoritative quest state permits the guarantee.

The first v10 production goal is technical modesty with unmistakable accountability. We now supply original **low-poly skinned previsualization figures**—a resident, a four-legged beast and a hovering wraith—each with named idle, walk, attack, gesture and speech clips. Their source is a self-contained portable glTF 2.0 asset. The authored meshes have explicit joint hierarchies and weighted vertices; the animations have sampled rotations keyed to those bones. This is a real step toward skeletal performances, but not a claim of actor likeness, completed facial rigs, retargeting, motion-capture polish or successful Unreal import. Until a Windows machine runs the engine import and validates asset names, the current source intentionally displays its earlier recognizable graybox shapes.

**Canonical performance rule:** a pose may communicate a witnessed decision; it must never manufacture one. An actor's saved observations, unpaid wages, work refusal, suspicion and consent remain in the authoritative economy and narrative rules. A cutscene cannot write a new truth through an animation event that has no gameplay provenance.

### 30.2 Three moving bodies, fifteen authored beginnings

**Resident** has seven bones, including a root, torso, head, two arms and two legs. Five keyed animation tracks provide a walking step, an explicit physical attack, an arm gesture, a small speaking head motion and an idle breath. The first imported residents can include the protagonist, Bes, Rheva, Mera and the six field scouts, but identical geometry is a *temporary shared rig*, not their eventual final character designs. Stable NPC identities, body choices and names survive the visual transition without a save migration.

**Beast** has seven bones and a deliberately lowered body silhouette: root, chest, head and four articulated limbs. Its locomotion and attack clips distinguish a ground creature from a walking town resident. It is the preview performer for Cinderjaw, Hollow Hound, Saltglass Crawler, Ashmaw and other close-fighting hostile actors; species-specific gait, anatomy, fangs, injuries, feeding and behavior remain future art and AI tasks rather than secretly completed work.

**Wraith** has five bones: central root and core, eye and two tendrils. Its pulses read differently from a ground hunter and align with the existing Watcher combat style. The source actor now selects skeletal asset and named clips according to its style. The asset importer must map both the skeletal mesh and all animation sequences to stable `/Game/UnmadeProduction/Characters/...` paths used in C++; a differently named import is not silently treated as successful.

In a first Unreal acceptance pass, each clip should be played while moving, during an attack, while returning to idle, on replay and after a level load. Missing assets must leave a visibly functional fallback. No voice recording, lip synchronization or dynamic facial performance has been produced in this source pass.

### 30.3 Places that have a shape of their own

A settlement's outskirts should be recognized without reading the sign. Ash Road has fossil ribs and a toppled waystone; Glass Cut has high prisms and a shard eye; Forgotten Bells has a suspended silent bell; Torn Archive exposes two displaced shelf-spines and an open folio; Saltglass Quay includes drowned timber piles and a wrecked hull; Blackslag Field has a chimney, broken kiln and slag heart. These are additional real Unreal runtime mesh actors with named tags, collision declared per structure and distinct orientation, built from engine-available primitive types. Their six configurations are no longer the same rectangular platform recited six times.

The color language is equally intentional. An exportable six-region physically based rendering swatch scene supplies ash-bone, pale-glass, bell patina, archive ink, salt foam and kiln slag materials with authored base-color, metalness and roughness values. Unreal scene construction attempts optional region-specific material assets only at documented paths; when they are not imported the geometry remains physically legible rather than disappearing. The glass and water scenes are still prototypes: true refraction, puddles, wetness layers, Niagara weather, high-quality modular meshes, LODs and cooked shader testing await Unreal Editor.

These ruins must leave civilian routes and wages intact. Cover is physical. A doorway used for debt evidence is not decorated into an unwalkable wall. The player must be able to return to a saved household with its previous outcomes, not a new scene replacing an older one with decorative props.

### 30.4 The creature tells the truth before striking

Unmade hostile pursuit now asks Unreal's navigation system for a traversable path, refreshes waypoints on a bounded interval, uses CharacterMovement collision, and will not pretend a swept straight line through solid walls is pathfinding. If navigation data is absent, only clear short local movement is permissible; full ruin pursuit then remains incomplete. Real NavMeshBoundsVolumes, runtime nav generation choices, cover and off-mesh links must be configured in the first Engine pass. Static source tests cannot prove a path exists in a cooked level.

More important for readable combat, the foe's strike now enters a **wind-up** instead of dealing instant damage on the same frame intent is selected. Ground beasts have a shorter warning; watchers telegraph their pulse for longer. The opponent displays an overhead warning and bright local light, giving the player a visible moment to guard, escape or move behind cover. Loss of direct line of sight cancels the pending impact. The actual hit remains a legitimate strike through the original cooldown, guard, armor and fracture model; no new instant-kill power or invisible damage privilege is introduced. Landing a player hit produces a brief target-side impact readout and light response; death still follows the existing no-loot/no-persistent-kill source contract.

This change is **not** a complete modern combat system. Per-species animation synchronized hit windows, positional audio, tactile controller feedback, weapon tracers, i-frames, navigation around other creatures, valid knockback, VFX pooling, real performance metrics and balanced challenge tiers remain explicit missing gates. The current overhead warnings are prototype signals, not a final accessible HUD. The final combat vocabulary must be readable at distance and under low-vision and reduced-motion modes.

### 30.5 Camera blocking is a promise of physical presence

The Unpaid Shift's scripted scene beats already had authored speakers, timed subtitles, Foley and directed camera cues. In v10, a physically nearby named speaker can now hold a temporary staged facing direction and request a keyed speech/gesture clip. The presentation rig checks the shot path for obstruction and slides a blocked camera inward before showing it. Skip and scene completion release the actor's pose and return the original view. The scene does not teleport a remote speaker, drag an NPC out of its workplace, or spend one mark merely by showing a line of dialogue.

The animation is expressly subordinate to the saved house, union/work-refusal state, Rheva witness, Mera consent and material transfers. A return encounter can change how Bes speaks because of a recognized outcome, not because the cinematic system invented a second copy of Bes. When a character's model is unavailable, the authored dialogue, movement continuity and subtitles remain functional. Full naturalistic staging still requires interior collision QA, reverse shots with viable eye lines, gesture-to-audio timing, dynamic actor marks, occlusion near narrow rooms, authored performance differences and Play-in-Editor inspection.

### 30.6 First-PC import and acceptance contract

1. Run the repository's `Scripts/build_skeletal_performance_kit.py --out <folder>` in a regular Python environment to produce the resident, beast, wraith and six-material glTF files. The field worker’s first scene makes the optional task personal. The witness’s later scene recognizes the reported guardian outcome. The leader’s returning conversation places the accord beside the repaired or disputed room. The rescued residents’ scene can occur only after the actual release and return conditions have been met. These scenes do not claim that a remote resident watched a fight they could not see.

Reserve, witness, repair and mediation events also enter the existing local memory system through nearby observers and clear sight. Trust and fear continue to affect native behavior. A witnessed repair can matter differently from a rumor of it. These additions do not overwrite old memories or create omniscient global reputation. The OPEN mysteries remain questions with bounded evidence, not rewards for accumulating familiarity.

### 37.5 Rewards that make a choice

Resolving a wing and claiming its return gives five insight. Four conditional relationship scenes can provide four more. Insight is finite and its accounting is exact. It does not replace local favor, trade marks, professions, wages or the original item economy.

The Duelist build adds attack while reducing armor and raising stamina cost. The Warden adds defense while lowering attack and movement speed. The Pathfinder extends reach and movement at a higher attack cost. A first build costs two insight; changing its type costs one. An already owned realm build can be reequipped freely, without changing the chosen weapon or learned technique. A traversal tool, crafted binding, local privilege or repair costs one. The journal shows these costs before spending, so an experiment can be a considered choice.

A tool opens the earned wing shortcut. A crafted binding adds two armor to the active realm build and extends a field kit’s danger suppression from twenty to thirty seconds. A privilege opens shared access and its bounded local specialty. Repair restores the work table and can support later mediation. These benefits affect routes, fighting, recovery and relationships rather than differing only in a reward label.

The new optional experience record validates discoveries, progress, scene prerequisites, day limits and spending against the earlier expedition and personal history. Failed writes restore state, relay attempts and world time before effects or rewards. Old saves have no obligation to contain the new fields. A damaged record is protected from being replaced by invented progress.

### 37.6 A journal with room to breathe

The interface now separates objectives, people and returns, evidence, the ten disciplines, and economy. A tracked objective stays visible while traveling. The map records discovered cities, visited chambers and completed wing stations; it does not display unseen room coordinates or personal options that have not been offered and heard. Choosing a map destination sets a bearing, not a teleport or a completed quest.

Settings provide adjustable text, camera sensitivity, sound volume, high contrast, reduced flashes, tutorial visibility and keyboard or controller prompts. Attack, guard, dodge, interaction and the learned technique can be rebound independently for keyboard and controller. Reserved and conflicting controls are refused with an explanation. Menu pages expose their own action groups and controller navigation, with gameplay input excluded while the menu is open.

A bed can pass six game hours after a second confirmation. The world’s obligations remain due. Recovery after defeat selects the nearest earned city’s safe platform without resetting story, inventory or people’s memories. Neither resting nor defeat appoints a different protagonist or a preferred ending.

The full native interface has not been rendered or playtested in Unreal. Font scaling, focus, capture, scrolling, controller behavior, moving support, navigation and every contact frame remain part of the engine acceptance. The downloadable editions preserve the complete earlier books and illustrations. They describe implemented source, portable checks and explicit production limits; they do not substitute for the first time the traveler actually walks these rooms.

### 38.1 Finding the next room

A player can still inspect the doorway. Arrival requires the earlier chamber and, for the arena, the chosen objective branch. Crossing a wall through a debug command does not create the missing history.

Ordinary opponents and the arena boss now use the same entered-room authority as their defeat record. ### 38.3 Choices the traveler can read

At the holding-chamber council, rescue and evidence remain two resolutions of the same expedition. Rescue requires both actual releases. Evidence requires all three records. ### 38.4 A journal that follows the journey

The tracker derives sixteen stages from the existing expedition, personal-quest and fieldwork records. It distinguishes the board from the frontier, individual unentered chambers from objective resolution, the boss from the return, and a witnessed return from an unclaimed reward. It names the local leader and witness when their actual conversations are next. It also distinguishes the personal offer, account, physical evidence and accord.

The optional wing has its own entrance, three fieldwork stations, guardian and claim station. A guardian's defeat does not silently claim the final reward. When those stages are resolved, the tracker identifies the journey as resolved and offers returning conversations, upgrades, repairs and mediation as optional work. It does not demand every finite purchase, convert familiar people into completion counters or force a single ethical ending.

The adventure journal now resolves the actual player equipment component before reading its modifiers. It no longer assumes the old single expedition kit is still the current weapon and armor. Equipment remains the authority for those independent slots and their passive technique; the learned active technique remains separately selected.

### 38.5 What the source verification establishes

The composed portable test follows fifty-four combinations across the nine realms: rescue with combat, evidence with a living surrender, both personal accords, and evidence journeys that release the captives only after the boss stands down. It restores the expedition, personal and experience records together at 1,944 checkpoints. It checks encounter eligibility, finite insight and service uses, permanent rewards, remembered scenes, repairs, mediation and refusal of corrupted progress. A separate session carries rewards through all nine realms to check that a later selection leaves earlier earned equipment intact.

The existing campaign, ten disciplines, later-realm chapters, ordinary combat, household, economy and consequence tests remain part of the full verification suite. Portable tests establish those source contracts; they do not prove a camera feels right, a doorway fits the character, a moving platform supports them or a controller focus path works in a viewport.

Unreal compilation, asset import, rendered interface review and engine playtesting remain deferred. The release preserves the full previous illustrated books and prototype assets. A source journey is now more coherent and better checked, while its rooms, performances and encounters still require the first real engine walkthrough before they can be called a finished playable world.
