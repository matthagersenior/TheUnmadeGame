# Chapter 43 — The Crossing That Refused a Single Answer

*One document travels across three places. Its consequences do not require
three cities to agree about what justice means.*

## Cinderhold: the debt and the living home

Bes's missing wages exist independently of the hero's petition. A private
claim protects a household without making the employer pay; a redacted public
claim demands an accounting without proving anyone guilty. The Unpaid Shift
retains its home, relief, labor refusal, town record, and actual restitution.
The Hearth Petition and its later relay cannot impersonate those systems.

Even after the relay has ended, a genuinely completed wage settlement may
change what the **player's record** says about the obligation. Neither Orrel
nor Mera learns such a settlement automatically. Their witnessed conversations
refer to the document actually placed in their hands, not an omniscient world
counter.

## Saltwake: responsibility does not follow the money alone

Mera, the courier and independent guarantor, is allowed to disagree with
the course chosen in Cinderhold. On the confidential route she asks how a
sealed record will be enforced without exposing people who needed shelter.
On the redacted public route she asks whether public scrutiny might create
another burden for the people it claims to protect.

After receiving the petition in person, a new civic question can be inspected
at Saltwake's real route. Its graybox record represents visible local
controversy, **not** a global change to trade prices, a new loan, or automatic
consent. It is absent before Mera personally receives the paper.

## The Crossings: access versus an accountable record

Orrel is not a judge who can erase consequences with one sentence. After
receiving Mera's physical reply, he creates one of two mutually exclusive,
optional, collision-enabled local places:

- With the **confidential petition**, a screened side-deck offers a
  protected route through the Crossings. Its physical appearance conveys
  protection while leaving unresolved responsibility visible in the records.
- With the **redacted public petition**, an evidence platform gives the
  traveler a place to inspect the civic obligation without publicly naming
  the refuge guests. A record is not a conviction.

These are source-world blockouts, not claims of a finished Unreal level.
Neither blocks the original roads, changes navigation attunement, summons
guards, grants equipment, or declares the city redeemed. The community may
still have separate outcomes from its original shelter and archive choices.
Both institutions and both memories must coexist.

## Why Nhal-Vey should care — without being told off-screen

Nhal-Vey insists that contradiction makes existence unsafe and only one
history may survive. The *player* can now bring an argument grounded in
life rather than in invented cosmic prophecy: a shelter guest has a right
not to be publicly exposed, while a wage claim can remain a legitimate,
documented obligation. Neither truth cancels the other.

Only after Orrel has received the reply can the Unanswered Road journal
show a branch-specific question challenging Nhal-Vey's premise. The public
claim asks how an account can remain open without exposing the person behind
it; the confidential claim asks whether an unpublicized duty may still be
enforceable. This is **an evidentiary and thematic alternative**, not free
boss damage, an extra final dialogue that bypasses consent, or a forced
ending. The main confrontation and the nine realms' independent histories
remain intact.

## Witness limits and later chapters

Bellwold's Hessa and Eillun's census keepers may eventually confront these
questions, but no existing person currently receives an unearned rumor or
knows of this filing merely because a flag was set. Any future extension
must show a verifiable conversation or carried record first. Public policies,
hearing dates, repayments, and any successor settlements must remain
consequences of specific people and actions, not universal switches.

The source adds physical route and investigation sites, branch-specific
local speech, a player-visible Nhal-Vey question, and deterministic rules.
Cinematic staging, pathfinding, collision and encounter accessibility still
need actual Unreal 5.8 acceptance and firsthand playtesting.
