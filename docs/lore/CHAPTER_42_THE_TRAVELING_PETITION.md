# Chapter 42 — The Petition That Had To Travel

*The city cannot know what has not arrived. Paper can be a burden, but not
a substitute for a person's voice.*

## A second decision: to carry what the city has accepted

After a traveler has heard Bes acknowledge the Hearth Petition, an actual
copy waits by his Cinderhold return station. Its text does not change the
player's prior private/public choice. Picking it up creates custody; it
does not summon a courier, absolve Rheva, or give a cross-realm resident
knowledge they could not have acquired.

The confidential copy retains the household and refuge boundary. The
redacted public copy carries the wage question but no guest names. Either
may be hand-carried across the dangerous road to Saltwake; the story is
optional, and the main road does not depend upon it.

## Mera's answer

The traveler must stand beside Mera, the actual Saltwake courier and
guarantor, to deliver the physical document. Her personal response differs.

On the **confidential route**, Mera insists that protection is necessary,
but asks whether a hidden record could let a creditor avoid accountability.

On the **redacted public route**, she supports recording the obligation
without exposing the guests, but fears public pressure will make vulnerable
households pay for an authority's failure.

Mera does not become a member of the worker's committee by a global flag;
she may disagree. She does not lend any additional marks. Even if the
existing independent guarantee was already offered, the receipt is
not automatically renewed by this conversation. She requires another
actual morning before handing over her considered signed reply. Time
alone does not teleport the letter into the player's possessions; another
in-person conversation must occur.

A sealed record or a redacted paper trail can become visible in Saltwake
only after the original document physically arrives in her hands.

## Orrel and the Crossings

The traveler then carries her reply to Orrel, the existing named bridge
keeper at the Crossings. He cannot speak about Mera's answer while it
is still in Saltwake or in the player's hands. An in-person delivery
records the crossing's reply and makes one of two different physical
archives visible:

- A confidential route produces a **sealed crossing archive** preserving
  the identity of protected travelers.
- A redacted public route produces a **civic crossing docket** requesting
  a formal accounting without publishing refuge names.

Orrel acknowledges receipt, not a final verdict. The builders' old debts,
Bes's unpaid wages, Mera's independent guarantee, community supplies,
rescue testimony and unfinished realities remain separate. The world
may remember the disagreement without forcing reconciliation.

## Continuity and the machinery of trust

The optional delivery is stored as four irreversible stages: collected
at Cinderhold, delivered to Mera, Mera's later reply collected, and
delivered to Orrel. Each stage carries an in-world day and is rejected
when time order or the parent petition does not match. The source-era
record is appended to schema-1 saves; missing old fields mean the
journey was never started, never that it completed off-screen.
Failed writes restore the previous custody. Once delivered, the
same stage cannot award duplicate reputation, loot or wages.

This content is implemented as source-state logic, physical actor
sites, journal directions, named reactions and portable tests.
Imported environments, professional audio, camera direction and
a complete Unreal 5.8 three-realm traversal remain acceptance work.
