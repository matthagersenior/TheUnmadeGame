# THE UNMADE — THE UNANSWERED ROAD
**The nine-realm campaign spine, first shipped in source on 10 October 2026**

## The core promise
A player is never asked to complete nine disconnected territories before being allowed to understand the final adversary. Every visited realm contributes a human answer to the same question: **what has been removed from the world, who made that removal useful, and who has the right to demand the missing thing back?** The player can reach the same truth through more than one civilization, and the people encountered remain real characters rather than lore dispensers.

The *main path* requires only opening testimony, one of two frontier first stories, one of three middle-realm first stories, one of two late-realm first stories, and the first Auvren trial. The resulting five investigative steps plus the confrontation are already made from existing actual quest decisions and atlas passages. No third-chapter Echo, guardian kill, observatory rite pairing, exhaustive map collection or paid content is a prerequisite. First-visit side consequences are still persistent and the optional material gives a richer interpretation to the ending. The main plot is **computed from actual quest state**, not simulated by a fresh progress toggle that can drift.

## The recurrent motif: the Unanswered Interval
The player first hears it as the silence after a bell. In one coast it is a missing sea, in one mountain an impossible sun, in a choir a missing singer, in a quarry a family erased from an origin. These are *not* coincidences delivered in identical quest descriptions. Each realm has a distinct lived use for the absence and a person who can defend the local explanation. Only later does the larger design become clear: someone has been turning unresolved histories into the scaffolding of reality itself.

The player should gradually learn that Nhal-Vey is not a monster responsible for everything bad; it is a consciousness recruited to suppress the questions which earlier generations refused to answer. This protects the moral ambiguity of the game. The world's injustices belong to people and institutions, too. Killing a creature cannot automatically release creditors from responsibility, restore an exploited choir member's consent, make erased workers' descendants whole, or settle the politics of an uncrowned court.

## Opening (Act 0) — A Bell That Had No Last Note
**Locations:** the Crossings, Bellwold, Paperhaven. **Recurrent perspectives:** a refugee asks for shelter without credentials; an archivist insists on preserving testimony; a traveler hears the same bell ring at two different times. The player may take a stand in the local dispute, discover two actual landmarks, or gain testimony by visiting two communities. No one opening alignment is canon or mandatory.

**First turning point:** Bellwold has a remembered bell that was never cast, while Paperhaven has a signed record for its absence. Neither witness is lying. A free-to-read physical thread marker appears on the familiar Reach road. The journal asks for either Rainward or Hearthward evidence rather than demanding both.

**Production scene:** The bell should fall silent for one *countable* interval; other town activity carries on. The player should not be forced through an unskippable cinematic. Two witnesses and a side conversation give three explanations; none explains the antagonist outright. A player who spends hours exploring still sees the thread in their journal.

## Act 1 — What the World Withheld
**Two equally real leads:** Saltwake in the Widowed Rain, or Cinderhold under the Borrowed Hearth. The navigational graph already has real Reach gateways to both, no high-level attunement required.

- **Saltwake:** Sella the navigator charts rainfall and discovers the sea hasn't simply drained: someone redacted a waterway from history. The ledger preserves dates from a later estuary. On completing the **first local story**, the player gains the sense that the silenced interval has been exported into a debt system. Next leads: Drevlach or Orravane.
- **Cinderhold:** Tarin the hearth-reader finds the exact absent beat in a flame nobody can own. It burns using a cost that an entire society has chosen not to name. On completing the first local story, the player has two leads: Orravane's load-bearing voices or Vathless's quarried ancestors.

**Mid-act character turn:** A trustworthy person defending a comforting lie must have good reasons (sheltering children, keeping a roof from falling); their story cannot be flattened into a villain signpost.

## Act 2 — What the Absences Cost
**Three alternatives, no prescribed order:**
- **Drevlach / Veynu:** debts with dates from future births reveal a financial infrastructure built from stolen histories.
- **Orravane / Yllesh:** the missing note literally holds homes suspended. Merely playing the forbidden note without preparation could kill innocents. The antagonist's shadow is a practical survival mechanism.
- **Vathless / Ghraet:** stone built from erased generations holds roofs up. Erasing the quarry would erase innocent households as well.

Finishing any one **first chapter** reveals that the missing history is being put to work; the civilizations have been paying for stability by making some people unaccountable or unreal. Player consequences are preserved, whether a shared route or evidence archive was chosen. An old save already completed in any of these regions is recognized. **A stronger response:** a local resident speaks their realm's unique witnessed evidence only once it becomes public. They do not learn revelations from other continents via magical global knowledge.

**Turn:** The player realizes that saving everyone immediately is not always possible. The first major ethical problem is not *who to defeat*, but how to stop the world's engine without making its current residents disappear.

## Act 3 — Who Deserves To Be Real
**Two alternatives based on genuinely connected atlas paths:**
- **Eillun / Nhel and Auvet:** people who were never born are alive, shelter one another, and disagree about formal recognition. The missing history doesn't make them meaningless. A truthful witness may refuse to give their name.
- **Tharniv / Serriv and Eliun:** a law nobody authorized continues to govern the orchard. The crown is empty because someone delegated the responsibility for impossible choices.

A player who approached through Drevlach naturally finds Eillun via the atlas; a player through Vathless can reach Tharniv. Orravane bridges both branches. Completing either first chapter reveals that the absence has a *legal or moral* mechanism. The player now understands why merely killing whoever forged the first lie would not restore a safe reality.

**Twist:** There may never have been a supreme chosen ruler or a single authoritative history. The system still operates because generations have treated "someone has to decide" as a universal law.

## Act 4 — The Witness Before Place
**Destination:** Auvren, reached from Eillun or Tharniv when earned atlas attunement permits. The first witness remembers the protagonist arriving *before* any arrival. This does not negate the player's chosen origin. It instead reveals that impossible people have been allowed to exist only under a system trained to consume unanswered questions.

**Reveal:** The first Auvren trial demonstrates a reliable safe threshold or a coexisting contradictory map. The player can ask an open question without first declaring one witness false. The journal changes its explicit objective to entering the northern arena. **No Echo story, optional guardian, or mastered ability becomes a hidden final-boss requirement.** The short main path is emotionally satisfying; the long optional route adds extra names and consequences to Nhal-Vey's memory signature.

## Act 5 — The Answer That Ate Its Question
Nhal-Vey is introduced as the consciousness tasked with preserving the world's contradictory architecture by eating questions. The first false victory destroys the mask the world's shared belief made visible. The second confrontation asks whether holding contradictory people together justifies a machine that can also erase their testimony. The player isn't asked to retcon earlier deeds: the boss uses a bounded signature from actual first-chapter choices and guardians spared, while the old save remains authoritative.

After the second defeat, two consciously confirmed outcomes lead into the previously implemented Held Morning or Many Mornings. The story becomes a new phase of living-world consequences, not a reset. A voluntary rematch is there for those who want it.

## Nonlinearity that remains coherent
The player can visit optional realms at any time permitted by earned crossings, and may have already finished first quests out of sequence. The journal evaluates the *highest contiguous main story act* based on the completion of at least one legitimate branch in each earlier category. It never deletes extra discoveries or invents unvisited ones. A guide can therefore recommend another route that is actually reachable at the player's current attunement, or explicitly say that the necessary crossing relic has not yet been earned.

The nine public Unanswered Road markers are situated close to the familiar local arrival area. Inspecting one provides its locally framed mystery before the first chapter and its longer, newly grounded evidence afterward. **Reading a marker does not force an oath, create a progress flag, grant an item, or farm XP.** Existing NPCs can share the same public revelation for their own home only after their local first quest has truly resolved.

## Interaction-first production specification
Every act still needs a dedicated first-PC walkable verification. The source currently supplies one authored physical marker per realm, opening/solved text, branching next-step route guidance with atlas attunement, journal integration, and a real final-boss access check. That is more than a lore-only outline, but is not equivalent to cinematic conversations, animated props, quest-tracking HUD, voiced narrators, full nine-region finished assets, or a playable certified UE build.

To make this an epic full RPG, future production must replace marker cubes with culturally appropriate props and directed performance, establish a recurring identifiable courier/letter motif, add visually referenced world-travel choices, create party/witness interactions, and permit lore being discovered through environment rather than text alone. The "Unsent Courier" is a **proposed recurring character**, not yet a spawned NPC or recorded voice; don't present the character as implemented. Advanced languages are discoveries, not translations automatically granted by this main spine.

### Future acts / post-victory
- Maintain a long-form optional friend or rival relationship with a witness who contradicts the protagonist's preferred account.
- Allow in-game readbacks of each "Unanswered Interval" record through the journal without restarting sidequests.
- Include one adaptive but bounded, locally observed antagonist rumor thread; differentiate firsthand accounts and speculative folklore.
- New changed-reality return quests can probe unresolved community disputes without magically resolving them.
- Never use a lore-proof checklist as a condition for real movement or a controller-layout prank.

## Implementation trace
`UnmadeCampaignSpineRules.h` contains the portable nine first-chapter beats, exact branching and atlas-based routing. `UnmadeCampaignWorld.cpp` creates story markers, computes evidence directly from actual `FrontierJourney`, `LaterRealmJourney`, village, landmark and local conflict state, and translates it into journal directions. `UnmadeFinalBoss.cpp` requires the intact campaign chain before starting the first confrontation; pre-existing saved final stages remain eligible. Optional Echo, guardians and observatories have no main-gate test.
