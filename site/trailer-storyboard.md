# THE UNMADE — A WORLD THAT REMEMBERS
## Cinematic trailer: director's production treatment (proposed)

**Status:** Preproduction creative brief. **There is no published cinematic trailer or verified Unreal gameplay footage.** This document defines what a credible, character-led cinematic should deliver—not a replacement for that film.

**Authority:** Derived from the spoiler-light `Authoring/cinematic_explainer_v1.json` (shots S01–S16) and the public game atlas. This marketing treatment is **not canon**. Specific staging, camera choices, dialogue and timings are proposals, subject to review against authoritative source. OPEN, DISPUTED and SEALED mysteries, final antagonist identity and the Held Morning / Many Mornings remain protected.

### Direction

**Runtime:** Aim for 75–90 seconds (a condensed edit of the source's 149.5-second, 16-shot explainer). **Delivery target:** 1920×1080, 24 fps, 16:9, original designed sound, accessible captions, with an optional 9:16 social cut.

**Visual premise:** A person makes one choice; a living world makes it matter. The trailer must show *cause and effect*, not merely nine disconnected postcards. Use grounded physical performance, cinematic environments, deliberate camera language, believable atmospheric motion and consistent character design. Avoid still-image pans, glowing glyph montages, repeated text cards and generic AI fantasy imagery.

**Structure:** A quiet, human-scale first act; impossible places revealed through movement; increasingly urgent physical choices; one witnessed return. Show danger without inventing a named boss or spoiling an ending.

The complete nine-realm structure remains represented in the public atlas; the cinematic does **not** need nine equal-length beauty shots. The Owed Estuary, Inverted Choir, Yesterday Quarry, Unclaimed City, Untitled Orchard and Before-Place are glimpsed through meaningful motion, not a checklist.

### Production requirements / acceptance tests

- **Motion:** Real character blocking, moving light/weather/cloth/props and motivated cameras. Static illustration with zoom is not acceptable for publication as a trailer.
- **Continuity:** One consistent traveler silhouette, scale, materials and environmental visual grammar across all shots. A traveler appearance is illustrative, not a fixed playable-character canon.
- **Meaning:** At least one readable observation → decision → witnessed reaction → persistent aftermath sequence, with no unsupported claim that gameplay currently works in Unreal.
- **Music and sound:** Original layered cinematic score and deliberate, physically motivated foley; licensed/owned materials only. Avoid a repetitive synthesized single-note bed.
- **Editing:** A clear rising rhythm, strong foreground silhouettes, visual legibility on a phone screen, no rapidly flashing cuts, no text walls over action.
- **Canon:** No resolving SEALED/OPEN/DISPUTED mysteries, revealing final ending mechanisms or treating optional arcs as required.
- **Truthfulness:** Label generated cinematics as concept visuals, never actual gameplay or Unreal captures. Do not publish as a completed trailer before a visual quality review.
- **Technical:** Consistent audio loudness, accessible captions, mobile playback, poster/thumbnail, landscape master and vertical-safe edit.

### Production dependencies and alternatives

A genuinely animated, consistent film needs either rendered 3D scenes/cameras (eventually Unreal), coherent high-quality image-to-video generation with shot continuity and editorial work, or commissioned animation. The connected Runway workspace currently does **not** expose video-generation models. **Do not silently substitute a low-quality procedural animation.**
